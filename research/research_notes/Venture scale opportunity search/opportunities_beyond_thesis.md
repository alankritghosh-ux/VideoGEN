## H1. C&I green open-access procurement decision + monthly settlement/bill-audit engine

### Takeaway
C&I open access is a large, fast-growing, charge-heavy market (18.7 GW cumulative by FY2024 per JMK/IEEFA, "30 GW" by Dec 2025 per an industry estimate) where consumers already pay developers, consultants and research houses to navigate state-specific charges, banking and approvals; the only free decision tool (CEEW) is frozen at FY2021-22 tariffs. Decision support is crowded with developer-funded "free advisory"; the under-served, recurring, data-rich job is the monthly energy-accounting / banking / DSM / bill reconciliation after signing.

### Cited Findings
- Cumulative C&I open access capacity reached 18.7 GW by end-FY2024; the market grew at a 46% CAGR FY2022–FY2024; Gujarat and Rajasthan account for >70% of recent installations; 28 of 29 states/UTs had adopted or drafted GEOA regulations as of Nov 2024 (Kerala the exception); DISCOMs are expected to meet a 15-day approval period; banking charges and settlement periods "are still ambiguous across states"; the GOAR portal "lacks synchronisation between SLDCs and DISCOMs and sometimes fails to recognise group captive models" — [JMK Research & IEEFA, Dec 2024, S-AN](https://jmkresearch.com/renewable-sector-published-reports/impact-of-green-energy-open-access-rules-2022/)
- Open access capacity "crossed 30 GW" by December 2025 (industry estimates; ~24.6 GW mid-2025, 27.9 GW Sept 2025); C&I solar additions ~15 GW in FY2026 (MNRE, +150%); CSS ~₹0.60/unit Karnataka, ~₹0.80–1.20/unit Maharashtra third-party; landed cost gap between best and worst industrial states ₹1.50–2.00/unit; application to first power 8–16 weeks — [Open Access Exchange blog, undated (data to May 2026), V-BLOG](https://www.openaccessexchange.com/blog/open-access-renewable-energy-india-2026-state-data/)
- State-wise indicative charges (₹/unit): Gujarat wheeling 0.60–0.90, CSS 0.50–0.80, banking 2–5% of banked energy; Maharashtra wheeling 0.80–1.20, CSS 1.50–2.50, additional surcharge 0.50–1.00, banking 5%; Tamil Nadu CSS 1.00–1.80; group captive requires ≥26% equity and ≥51% consumption and "most states exempt group captive consumers from CSS and additional surcharge", saving ₹1.50–2.50/unit vs third-party — [SurgePV guide, published 27 May 2026, reviewed 8 Aug 2026, V-BLOG](https://www.surgepv.com/solar-compliance/india/guides/open-access)
- Worked savings examples: Manesar 5 MW PPA, DISCOM ₹7.32/unit vs landed ₹4.78/unit (₹2.44/unit); a 1.8 MW (60% of load) consumer cut annual bill from ₹18.7 Cr to ₹11.4 Cr with "payback under 6 months on advisory and connection costs" (i.e., advisory is a paid line item, amount not given) — [Hero Future Energies blog, 8 Jun 2026, V-BLOG](https://www.herofutureenergies.com/blog/solar-open-access-for-commercial-industrial-consumers-india/)
- GEOA recurring charges listed: transmission, wheeling, CSS (third-party only), banking, LDC fees and "deviation settlement penalties: usage-based, under DSM"; GEOA threshold 100 kW; 15-day approval cap — [Sunphotonics, undated (2025/26), V-BLOG](https://sunphotonics.com/green-energy-open-access-apply-process-2025/)
- CEEW-CEF's free Open Access Landed Cost Calculator compares DISCOM tariff vs OA landed cost for a 1 MW / 80% LF consumer across states/modes/fuels, but "all open access charges have been determined based on tariff orders and regulations applicable for FY 2021-22 and are as recent as December 2021"; it assumes 15% banking and one-month short-term OA; it does not do scheduling, DSM or bill reconciliation — [CEEW-CEF methodology note, Dec 2021 data, S-AN](https://www.ceew.in/cef/shared/CEEW-CEF-Open-access-landed-cost-calculator-methodology.pdf)
- JMK sells its "Open Access C&I RE Market in India" report at "INR 2 lacs + 18% GST (USD 2700)" with quarterly updates and a landed-cost section "as of March 2026" — evidence that buyers pay for OA market/charges data — [JMK Research product page, accessed 2026-10-17, P-CO](https://jmkresearch.com/open-access-ci-re-market-in-india/)
- Open Access Exchange offers "end-to-end OA compliance and regulatory management" and a facility-specific feasibility model "at no cost" (developer-subsidised advisory) — [Open Access Exchange, V-BLOG](https://www.openaccessexchange.com/blog/open-access-renewable-energy-india-2026-state-data/)
- Crisil estimates cross-subsidy and additional surcharges form 65–70% of total open access charges on average — [Crisil press release, Sept 2021, S-AN](https://www.crisil.com/en/home/newsroom/press-releases/2021/09/draft-open-access-norms-can-be-a-tailwind-for-new-renewable-projects.html)
- Tracxn (India, feeds "Electricity Exchange and Trading" + "Services for Energy Storage"): 205 companies; the top-funded are IEX (public) and Kalpa Power (OA developer, ~$1.76M equity); no company in the top 20 is an OA settlement/optimisation software vendor — [Tracxn, TRX-EST](https://platform.tracxn.com/a/s/query/t/companiescovered/t/all?h=845a3c7c83ceab3ed45f8df1fee66982d8b5668dc4c66ef23117f314bc59750a&s=sort%3Dcompanytotalequityfunding%7Corder%3DDESC&o=tracxn-mcp&trx_rcid=e7a215b0-0650-401e-b3b2-473f4f0786a7)

### Inferences
- Initial customer: multi-site manufacturers (textiles, auto components, chemicals, cement grinding units) in Maharashtra/Karnataka/Tamil Nadu with 1–20 MW contracted demand already on OA or group captive; economic buyer: plant head / head of energy procurement / CFO. [INF]
- Painful workflow: every month the consumer receives a DISCOM bill, a generator invoice, SLDC energy-accounting statements and banking/lapse statements; charges change with each SERC tariff order; disputes on banking lapse, DSM penalties and CSS application are reconciled in spreadsheets. [INF, supported by JMK's "ambiguous banking" finding and Sunphotonics' list of charges]
- Proposed product: (1) a settlement engine that ingests the customer's exported bills/SLDC statements and recomputes what they should have paid under the applicable tariff order (detects over-billing, mis-applied CSS/AS, banking lapse errors), and (2) a procurement optimiser (group captive vs third-party vs rooftop vs grid, state, duration, banking share) calibrated to the customer's 15-minute load profile. Output: monthly "recoverable amount" report and signed-off bill audit; annual sourcing plan. [INF]
- Required data: customer's bills, load data (available from DISCOM/AMR exports), SERC tariff orders (public PDFs), SLDC statements (customer-provided). All non-privileged. Validation: backtest on 12–24 months of the customer's historical bills and show recovered ₹. [INF]
- Prototype: 6–10 weeks for one state (Maharashtra or Karnataka) with an AI coding agent; cost mostly founder time plus <₹50k cloud. Pricing: % of recovered overcharges (success fee) for the audit, then ₹2–5 lakh/yr/site SaaS for ongoing settlement; comparable to JMK's ₹2 lakh report price as a willingness-to-pay anchor. [INF]
- Defensibility vs frontier models: per-state tariff-order rule libraries, DISCOM billing quirks, and a growing corpus of real bill/settlement pairs; the compounding asset is the audited history across customers. [INF]
- Expansion: into scheduling/forecasting for the generator side (this touches the founder's thesis), BESS co-location decisions (H2), and CCTS/Scope-2 reporting (H6). [INF]
- Main risks: developers give advisory free as a sales tool; buyers may expect the generator to handle settlement; state rules change (regulatory risk cuts both ways: it creates recurring need). [INF]

### Gaps
- No published consultant fee benchmark for OA advisory (searched; "not found"). The Hero Future Energies case says advisory had <6-month payback but gives no amount.
- Number of C&I OA consumers and the group-captive vs third-party split are behind the JMK paywall.
- No source found on how often OA consumers are over-billed or how DSM penalties are actually applied to consumers (versus generators) under state regulations.
- Search budget exhausted before testing whether any Indian SaaS already does OA bill reconciliation.

## H2. BESS dispatch & bidding optimiser for India's DAM/RTM + contracted-availability fleet

### Takeaway
India tendered ~102 GWh of storage in 2025 alone, merchant arbitrage became profitable in 2024 per Ember, RTM is now 34% of IEX volumes, and 72% of IEX day-ahead volume is already bid via APIs, yet neither Tracxn nor trade press surfaced an Indian bid/dispatch optimisation vendor. The catch is that most awarded Indian BESS are availability-paid (DISCOM dispatches and supplies charging energy), so the near-term buyer is the developer's O&M/contract-compliance team, with merchant optimisation a later upsell.

### Cited Findings
- Merchant BESS: market revenue rose from ~INR 0.5 million/MWh (2015) to INR 2.4 million/MWh (2025); annualised cost of a 2024-vintage system ~INR 1.7 million/MWh; revenue first exceeded cost in 2024; DAM-only IRR ~17%, up to ~24% with ancillary services; DAM arbitrage is 80–85% of earnings; LCOS ~INR 4.5/kWh; break-even selling price ~INR 7.56/kWh when charging at ~INR 2.6/kWh — [Ember, 4 Aug 2025, S-AN](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/the-age-of-storage-batteries-primed-for-indias-power-markets/chapter-3-the-economics-of-merchant-bess/)
- DAM prices hit the INR 10/kWh cap in one of every six hours 2022–2024; average summer midday prices fell ~20% 2022–2024; prices near zero on some 2025 summer days — [Ember, 5 Aug 2025, S-AN](https://ember-energy.org/latest-updates/battery-storage-operations-in-indias-power-exchanges-became-profitable-for-the-first-time-in-2024/)
- Counter-evidence: a market-based study of IEX DAM 2015–2023 finds energy arbitrage "would have generally fallen short of a typical hurdle rate of return of 10% for all storage options in the majority of historical years" (worked example ~INR 3.65 million gross/MW/yr, ~INR 2.05 million after fixed O&M) — [Suski et al., Journal of Energy Storage 2025, S-AN (abstract via search summary; full text not fetched)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352152X25045177)
- IEX Q1 FY27 (24 Jul 2026): RTM volume 54.9 BU in FY26 (+41%), 34% of volumes; Q1 FY27 RTM 16.0 BU (+23%); management cites "arbitrage opportunity of INR 4-5 per kWh, with 500+ cycles per year"; standalone BESS tariffs fell from INR 10.8 lakh/MW/month (Apr 2022) to INR 1.8–1.9 lakh by early 2026; near-zero solar-hour prices on ~31 days in Q1 FY27; "72% of cleared Integrated Day-Ahead Market volume came from members using bidding APIs"; peak-power contracts for BESS await regulatory approval; market coupling litigation (CERC Jul 2025 order → APTEL Feb 2026 → Supreme Court; draft regulations Apr 2026) — [Investing.com summary of IEX investor slides, 24 Jul 2026, S-PRESS](https://in.investing.com/news/company-news/iex-q1-fy27-slides-rtm-surge-drives-12-profit-growth-amid-coupling-risk-93CH-5513657)
- Pipeline: "Around 102 GWh was tendered across roughly 69 tenders in 2025 alone"; five projects totalling ~547 MWh commissioned in 2025; cumulative installed storage ~758 MWh at end-2025; GUVNL Phase-IX (awarded Aug 2026) 450 MW/900 MWh at ~₹2.32 lakh/MW/month with VGF ₹18 lakh/MWh (NLC India Renewables 275 MW, Sun Drops 175 MW); UPPCL 4-hour (May 2026) 375 MW/1,500 MWh at ~₹6.45–6.46/kWh energy tariff; in capacity-charge standalone deals "the DISCOM pays for asset availability and supplies the charging energy"; BOO terms typically 12–15 years — [Tenderkosh blog, 26 Apr 2026, updated 17/18 Aug 2026, V-BLOG](https://blog.tenderkosh.com/bess-tenders-india-2026/)
- Week of 20–26 Sep 2026: NTPC 400 MWh Mouda EPC re-tender (after terminating GR Infraprojects' ₹413.37 Cr contract), NGEL Khavda 1,100 MW/3,300 MWh EPC (bids 27 Oct 2026), TGGENCO Maheswaram VGF tender, MSEDCL BESS services tender; SJVN 265 MW/530 MWh Haryana won by NLC India Renewables at ₹2.35 lakh/MW/month — [Urjapod weekly brief, 28 Sep 2026, S-PRESS](https://www.urjapod.com/post/india-bess-tenders-weekly-brief-sept-20-26-2026)
- IEEFA (Apr 2025): 9.33 GW of standalone BESS tendered across 20 tenders to Mar 2025, operational 0.13 GW, pipeline 3.1 GW; VGF-backed tariffs as low as Rs219,001–221,100/MW/month (~40% below non-VGF); NTPC issued six BESS tenders totalling 5.75 GW since 2022; VGF scheme capped at 13.2 GWh; CEA estimates 320 GW/2,380 GWh storage need by 2047 — [IEEFA, "The Standalone Energy Storage Market in India", Apr 2025, S-AN (PDF extracted)](https://ieefa.org/sites/default/files/2025-04/The%20Standalone%20Energy%20Storage%20Market%20in%20India_April%202025.pdf)
- SECI auction mechanics (vendor explainer): developer builds, owns and operates; remuneration is availability (fixed) + energy delivery (variable); contracts typically require 250–350 cycles/yr and 95–97% availability with liquidated damages; VGF outlay ₹3,760 Cr Phase 1 and ₹9,400 Cr Phase 2 — [SilicIndia Energies, 8 May 2026, V-BLOG](https://www.silicindiaenergies.com/news/seci-bess-tenders/)
- SECI issued an 800 MW/3,200 MWh standalone BESS RfS (SECI-ESS-IV) under tariff-based bidding, RfS dated 1 Oct 2026 — [SolarQuarter, 5 Oct 2026, S-PRESS (page returned 429/403 on fetch; figure from search snippet)](https://solarquarter.com/2026/10/05/seci-issues-800-mw-3200-mwh-standalone-bess-tender-under-tariff-based-competitive-bidding/)
- Global vendors with bid optimisation: Fluence Mosaic, PCI BatteryTrader, Ascend SmartBidder; none described as India/IEX-focused; a 2025 "energy storage software companies to watch" list contains one Indian firm (Xbattery, a BMS maker, not an optimiser) — [StartUs Insights, updated 9 Jul 2025, V-BLOG](https://www.startus-insights.com/innovators-guide/energy-storage-software-companies/); [Fluence Mosaic, P-CO](https://fluenceenergy.com/mosaic-intelligent-bidding-software/)
- Tracxn: among 205 Indian companies in electricity-trading/storage-services feeds, none of the top 20 by funding is a BESS bidding/dispatch optimiser; shatterdome (founded 2025, "AI software for managing distributed energy assets", $3.5M seed May 2026) and Taqanal Energy ("cloud based solution for EV and BESS", $2.5M) are the closest adjacent — [Tracxn, TRX-EST](https://platform.tracxn.com/a/s/query/t/companiescovered/t/all?h=0e55748797b0f19b262a43c312682f306d3a1bba90e6cb0dfd7190a49b2c937e&s=sort%3Dcompanytotalequityfunding%7Corder%3DDESC&o=tracxn-mcp&trx_rcid=427ca62c-e6ab-46b4-be95-affa9c491595)

### Inferences
- Initial customer: mid-size winners of VGF tenders who lack a trading desk (Oriana Power, Pace Digitek, Sun Drops Energia, Sarus, HG Infra, Advait Infratech, KCC Buildcon, Agastya, MKC — names from IEEFA/Tenderkosh award lists); economic buyer: asset-management / O&M head, later the trading head. Large incumbents (Tata Power, JSW, Greenko, NTPC) have in-house desks and are late targets. [INF]
- Painful workflow today: contract availability tracking (cycle counting, SoC/degradation vs 95–97% availability clauses, LD exposure) in spreadsheets; for the few merchant/partly-merchant assets, DAM/RTM bids built manually from price history. [INF]
- Product: (a) contract-compliance and dispatch-scheduling engine (cycle budgeting, availability forecasting, LD-risk alerts, warranty-degradation tracking) built on the asset's EMS/SCADA exports; (b) DAM/RTM/ancillary bid optimiser with backtests against public IEX price series. Output: daily dispatch plan + bid file; monthly availability certificate. [INF]
- Data: IEX DAM/RTM 15-minute prices are public (IEX site, energymap.in); tender terms are public RfS documents; asset telemetry comes from the customer. Validation: backtest the optimiser on 2022–2026 IEX prices with published LCOS/RTE assumptions; compare with Ember's INR 2.4m/MWh revenue benchmark. [INF]
- Prototype: 6–8 weeks for the backtest + bid engine; 3–4 months for an EMS-integrated compliance module. Pricing: ₹/MW/month (e.g., ₹2,000–5,000/MW/month ≈ 1–2.5% of a ₹2.2 lakh/MW/month capacity charge) or revenue share on merchant uplift. [INF]
- Risks: most 2025–26 awards are availability contracts where the DISCOM/SLDC issues dispatch instructions, limiting optimisation headroom; market coupling litigation could change price formation; large OEMs (Fluence, Tesla, Sungrow) bundle EMS/trading; global optimisers may enter when merchant volume grows. [INF]
- Defensibility: India-specific contract templates (SECI/NTPC/GUVNL BESPA clauses), degradation models calibrated per site, and accumulated dispatch-vs-outcome data. [INF]

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the SECI-ESS-IV RfS to confirm whether merchant participation/co-optimisation is allowed under current standalone contracts.
- No per-MW revenue figure for RTM-specific arbitrage; Ember's numbers are DAM-based and its INR 2.4m vs 3.2–3.3m/MWh figures are not reconciled in its own text.
- No CERC/IEX document fetched on BESS participation rules in RTM/ancillary markets (search budget exhausted).
- No evidence on what Indian developers currently pay for dispatch/trading services (e.g., trader margins, QCAs for BESS).

## H3. PM Surya Ghar vendor operations: DISCOM-approval tracking, delay prediction and subsidy-claim management

### Takeaway
PM Surya Ghar has ~26.2 lakh installations (to 19 Mar 2026) running at ~2 lakh/month with ₹17,968 Cr subsidy disbursed, a vendor base being actively disciplined by MNRE (66 vendors deactivated Aug 2026; 60-day application-holding rule; vendor rating), and a 7-step DISCOM/net-meter/DBT workflow that takes 6–20 weeks depending on state. Vendors already pay ₹46k–1.07 lakh/yr for design/proposal software, but the operational bottleneck (approvals, inspections, subsidy release) is served only by generic CRMs; MNRE opened portal API access to high-volume vendors in June 2026.

### Cited Findings
- 26,19,879 rooftop systems installed as of 19 Mar 2026 (≈5.8 lakh in 2024, ≈15 lakh in 2025, 5.3 lakh Jan–19 Mar 2026, i.e., ~2 lakh/month); ₹17,967.53 Cr central financial assistance disbursed; target 1 crore by Mar 2027; Vendor Rating Programme (Jul 2024): vendors need ≥50 portal installations to be rated, 50–250 rated on consumer feedback, >250 also on sample site inspections and techno-financial assessment; 2026 portal changes include a DISCOM transformer-loading dashboard (May), auto-fill (June) and "API access for high-volume empanelled vendors (June)" — [Heaven Green Energy summary citing PIB PRID=2244670, published 6 Jun 2026, updated 7 Aug 2026, V-BLOG citing P-OFF (PIB page itself returned 403)](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/pm-suryaghar-updates)
- MNRE deactivated 66 PM Surya Ghar vendors for one month over applications held at "Agreement Uploaded" during Apr–Jun 2026 far above their demonstrated installation capacity; vendors advised not to hold applications >60 days without valid reason — [IndianPSU, 27 Aug 2026, S-PRESS](https://indianpsu.com/mnre-deactivates-66-pm-surya-ghar-vendors-pending-solar-applications/)
- Workflow and field timelines (Apr 2026): DISCOM feasibility 3–15 working days; installer scheduling 7–21 days; net-metering application and inspection 7–45 working days ("most variable stage"); net meter install 5–21 days; commissioning certificate 3–10 days; DBT target 30 working days after bank validation. State totals: Gujarat/Punjab 6–8 weeks; Delhi 7–10; Haryana 8–12; Rajasthan 10–14; UP/MP 12–20 weeks. Delay causes: Aadhaar/bank name mismatch, document mismatch, earthing/inverter/non-ALMM objections at inspection, inspector backlog in urban circles, nodal-agency batch processing, stale portal status — [SolarSahi, 12 Apr 2026, V-BLOG](https://solarsahi.com/learn/subsidy-guides/pm-surya-ghar-subsidy-processing-time-2026/)
- Vendor software prices (India): Arka360 Lite ₹46,000/yr (360 residential projects/yr), Basic ₹70,000/yr (unlimited), Premium ₹1,00,000/yr (+18% GST, ₹10,000 onboarding); PM Surya Ghar workflow and monitoring not mentioned on the pricing page — [Arka360 pricing page, accessed 2026-10-17, P-CO](https://www.arka360.com/pricing-in)
- Competitive set (Aug 2026): SurgePV ~₹1.07 lakh/user/yr (team) with "automatic subsidy calculation from the sanctioned system size"; Solar Ladder (quote-only) "tracks DISCOM steps in its pipeline"; QuickEstimate ~₹36,000/user/yr CRM; PVsyst ~₹73,000/yr; HelioScope/Aurora $1,620–2,640/yr; OpenSolar free — [Heaven Green Energy (sister company of SurgePV; conflicted), 3 Aug 2026, V-BLOG](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/best-solar-software-india)
- Tracxn (India, feed "Solar Sales and Design Software"): 21 companies; Solar Ladder (~$2.3M raised, 27 employees), AhaSolar (public, 85 employees), PM Solar Mart ("digital workflows for solar installation and subsidy management", unfunded), several unfunded CRMs; two deadpooled — [Tracxn, TRX-EST](https://platform.tracxn.com/a/s/query/t/companiescovered/t/all?h=1aa3300922fb01c0a8b7dde3a297df57c005d9575f97bebe521074a8cbc9d94d&s=sort%3Dcompanytotalequityfunding%7Corder%3DDESC&o=tracxn-mcp&trx_rcid=94793028-a4f5-4778-ba7d-7950cd295846)

### Inferences
- Initial customer: empanelled vendors with 50–500 installations/quarter in slow states (UP, MP, Rajasthan, Maharashtra) whose rating and cash flow depend on cycle time; economic buyer: vendor owner. Secondary buyer: rooftop financiers (banks under the scheme's loan window) who want visibility on subsidy-release risk. [INF]
- Product: ingest the vendor's portal exports (and, for high-volume vendors, the June-2026 API) to (a) predict days-to-subsidy per application by DISCOM circle and stage, (b) flag applications likely to breach the 60-day rule or fail inspection (ALMM/earthing/doc mismatch), (c) prioritise field work, and (d) produce the MNRE rating-ready evidence pack. This is "estimate/predict/decide" work on routine exports, not a design tool. [INF]
- Validation without privileged access: back-test stage-duration predictions on a vendor's historical application log; publish circle-level cycle-time benchmarks from aggregated customer data. [INF]
- Prototype: 4–6 weeks (CSV/portal-scrape ingestion + survival-model on stage durations); cost minimal. Pricing: ₹500–1,000 per application or ₹3–8k/month per vendor, below Arka360's ₹46k/yr entry tier. [INF]
- Risks: MNRE could build these features into the national portal (it already ships SMS alerts, status dashboards); the vendor market is fragmented and price-sensitive; design-software incumbents (Arka360, SurgePV, Solar Ladder) can add workflow modules. [INF]
- Defensibility: cross-vendor DISCOM-circle benchmark dataset and rejection-reason taxonomy that improves with volume; vendor rating interactions. Modest. [INF]

### Gaps
- Official count of registered/empanelled vendors not found (pmsuryaghar.gov.in and PIB blocked; secondary pages did not state it).
- No evidence of what vendors spend on operations/CRM tooling beyond design software list prices.
- Installation totals conflict across secondary sources (26.2 lakh by Mar 2026 vs "33 lakh by May 2026" vs "5.95 million households by Sept 2026" in search snippets); only the PIB-cited 19 Mar 2026 figure is treated as reliable here.

## H4. DISCOM feeder/DT energy accounting and theft-lead scoring from smart-meter exports (sold via AMISPs, ISGF pilots and private DISCOMs)

### Takeaway
RDSS has 5.7–6.0 crore smart meters installed against 20.33 crore sanctioned (deadline pushed to Mar 2028), AT&C losses were 15–16% with ₹90,000 Cr+ annual revenue loss, and multiple Indian case studies show AI theft-lead scoring produces 41–57% booking rates from flagged cases. The problem and data are real, but the buyer is a state DISCOM procured via L1 tenders and AMISP TOTEX contracts; a startup's realistic path is as a subcontractor/analytics layer to AMISPs (Kimbal, Probus, Fluentgrid, Esyasoft) or to private DISCOMs, which Bidgely has already partly occupied.

### Cited Findings
- 6,00,36,379 smart meters installed under RDSS as of Aug 2026 vs 20.33 crore sanctioned and a 25 crore target; deadline March 2028; state table (Maharashtra 1.34 crore, UP 1.01 crore, Gujarat 60.8 lakh, Assam 56.9 lakh, Rajasthan 52.2 lakh) — [Kimbal (AMISP) blog citing RDSS MIS dashboard, 17 Aug 2026, V-BLOG citing P-OFF](https://kimbal.io/blog/rdss-scheme-state-wise-update/)
- 7.24 crore smart meters installed nationally as of 30 Jun 2026 (5.73 crore under RDSS = 28.2% progress); Gujarat 52.01 lakh of >1.67 crore sanctioned; meeting the 2028 deadline would need ~6.5 lakh installations/day; consumer distrust over prepaid billing — [The Secretariat, 25 Aug 2026, S-PRESS](https://thesecretariat.in/article/smart-meter-mission-loses-momentum-as-installation-targets-slip)
- AMISP TOTEX model: costs financed over 10 years, paid monthly over 93 months as (award rate − subsidy)/93; single-phase consumer meter median award rate Rs 8,839 (range 7,236–12,118); DT meter median Rs 28,954; feeder meter median Rs 48,529; monthly AMISP charge Rs 60 (WB) to Rs 146 (MP); as of Mar 2025 only 13.4 million consumer meters (5% of target) were communicating; ~87,000 feeder meters installed and ~75,000 communicating; ~0.44 million DT meters (3%) communicating; "bid and contract information is confidential" — [Prayas Energy Group, 16 May 2025, S-AN](https://energy.prayaspune.org/power-perspectives/smart-metering-in-india-a-work-in-progress)

## H5. CBAM embedded-emissions calculation, Monitoring Methodology Document and verifier-ready data packs for Indian steel/aluminium/cement exporters

### Takeaway

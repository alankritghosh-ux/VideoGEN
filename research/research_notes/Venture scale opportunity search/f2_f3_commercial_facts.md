## F2-Q1. Karnataka open-access regime (KERC/KPTCL-SLDC/BESCOM): rules, charges, process

### Takeaway
Karnataka re-based its OA regime in 2025 (KERC OA Regulations 2025 after the High Court struck down the 2022 GEOA rules): 100 kW threshold, SLDC as application nodal agency, **monthly banking with no carry-forward**, standby at 125%, and charge-setting by tariff order. BESCOM asked for a ₹1.65/kWh additional surcharge from 1 Apr 2026 (on a ₹1,028 Cr stranded-cost claim) and KERC approved ~₹0.40/kWh (down from ₹0.82 in FY26), not applicable to captive drawal, so the charge stack is contested every year, which is the recurring need F2 monetises. The primary Karnataka SLDC spreadsheet shows **556 generators / 4,924 MW** under wheeling-and-banking agreements statewide (as on 09.07.2024) and roughly **900+ distinct consumer organisations** in the published approved-consumer lists.

### Cited Findings
- KERC (Terms and Conditions for Open Access) Regulations, 2025 replace the 2022 Green Energy OA rules struck down by the Karnataka High Court; eligibility is captive plants under s.9 and consumers with HT contract demand or LT sanctioned load ≥100 kW; the Karnataka SLDC handles OA applications; **monthly banking** for solar/wind/mini-hydel, excess injected energy must be used within the same month, no carry-forward (published 2 Apr 2025) — [SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2025/04/02/karnataka-introduces-new-open-access-regulations-2025-to-boost-power-accessibility-and-renewable-energy/) **VF (secondary summary of regulation)**. DI: F2's Karnataka rule library must implement month-level banking/lapse logic.
- Same regulations per Renewable Watch (25 Apr 2025): banking charge **8% or as decided by the Commission**, banking monthly; wheeling/CSS/additional surcharge set by periodic tariff orders; transmission charges computed monthly from TTSC and contracted capacity with different formulas for short-term vs medium/long-term; **standby power billed at 125%** of normal energy rates; day-ahead OA applications by 13:00, decision by 15:00, payment by 17:00; agreements within 20 working days or OA lapses; short-term under-utilisation >4 h must be notified, charges stay payable on reserved capacity; relinquishment compensation = 3 months of charges; SLDC may cancel capacity if under-utilisation occurs >2 times a month for >2 h — [Renewable Watch](https://renewablewatch.in/2025/04/25/karnataka-announces-open-access-regulations-2025/) **IC** (consistent with SolarQuarter on structure; the 8% figure appears only here). DI: multiple charge-events per month (under-utilisation, standby, relinquishment) are exactly the items a settlement engine recomputes.
- Historical Karnataka W&B charges: KERC 2018 raised wheeling for OA from **5% to 25% of normal transmission charges**; banking **2% in kind**, no cash (Mercom, 23 May 2018) — [Mercom](https://www.mercomindia.com/wheeling-charges-karnataka-five-fold) **VF (fetched)**. KERC's 17 Aug 2023 directive keeps the 7 Sep 2021 interim-order charges for non-REC projects that applied for W&B between 1 Apr 2018 and the GEOA cut-offs (STOA applications ≤13 Jan 2023, LTOA ≤2 Jan 2023), with **annual banking at 2% of injected energy**, subject to a pending Supreme Court matter — [SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2023/08/17/kerc-issues-vital-directive-on-wheeling-charges-and-banking-for-renewable-power-projects/) **VF**. DI: Karnataka has at least two coexisting banking regimes (legacy annual 2% in-kind vs new monthly ~8%), i.e., consumers on legacy WBAs and GEOA contracts are billed on different rules; "which regime applies to this consumer-generator pair" is a core audit question.
- BESCOM petitioned KERC (filed 24 Aug 2025) for an **additional surcharge of ₹1.65/kWh** on OA consumers from 1 Apr 2026, computed as estimated annual stranded-power cost **₹1,028.29 crore** divided by total OA energy (MU) for FY2024-25, with inputs from the SLDC and other ESCOMs — [SolarQuarter, 14 Oct 2025](https://solarquarter.com/2025/10/14/bescom-seeks-kerc-approval-to-levy-%e2%82%b91-65-per-unit-additional-surcharge-on-open-access-consumers-in-karnataka/) **VF**. Implied OA energy ≈ ₹1,028.29 Cr ÷ ₹1.65/kWh ≈ **6,230 MU/yr in BESCOM's area** (my arithmetic) **EST**. KERC public hearing 13 Nov 2025 covering OA consumers of all five ESCOMs — [SolarQuarter, 4 Nov 2025](https://solarquarter.com/2025/11/04/kerc-to-hold-public-hearing-on-proposed-additional-surcharge-for-open-access-power-consumers-in-karnataka/) **VF**.
- Outcome: KERC approved a reduced additional surcharge of **₹0.40/kWh from March 2026** until the FY2027 determination, versus **₹0.82/kWh in force for FY2026**; it does not apply to energy drawn for captive consumption — Mercom "Karnataka Halves Additional Surcharge for Open Access Power" (page 404 on fetch; figures from search snippet) [Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/karnataka-halves-additional-surcharge-for-open-access-power) **IC** (corroborated by the BESCOM-petition articles above and the SolarQuarter search summary). DI: the FY26→FY27 swing (₹0.82 → ₹0.40, vs ₹1.65 asked) is ~₹25 Cr/yr on 6,000 MU; mis-application of the captive exemption is an audit line item.
- KERC notified the Roadmap for Reducing Cross-Subsidy and Cross-Subsidy Surcharge Regulations, 2026 (7 Mar 2026): most categories incl. industrial/commercial already within ±20% of cost of supply; CSS methodology follows national tariff policy; no CSS ₹/kWh stated — [Energetica India](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/kerc-issues-2026-regulations-notifies-roadmap-to-gradually-reduce-cross-subsidy-and-cross-subsidy-surcharge) **VF**. Prior note's ~₹0.60/unit CSS (Open Access Exchange blog) remains the only CSS figure found **EST**.
- KERC order of 31 Mar 2026 extended the Discounted Energy Rate Scheme (DERS) for HT-2a/2b/2c and LT-3a/LT-5 to FY2026-27 and FY2027-28 and **removed the 2025 condition that consumers surrender OA/wheeling** to get the discount; OA participants cannot claim the extra 20% contract-demand headroom — [SolarQuarter, 2 Apr 2026](https://solarquarter.com/2026/04/02/kerc-extends-discounted-energy-rate-scheme-till-fy28-eases-open-access-rules-for-consumers-in-karnataka/) **VF**. DI: "DERS vs OA" is a live optimisation question for every HT consumer in Karnataka (procurement module).
- **Primary data (Karnataka SLDC, KPTCL):** the SLDC "Wheeling & Banking Generators/Consumers" page publishes per-ESCOM approved-consumer lists and an IPP spreadsheet — [kptclsldc.in/WBUpload.aspx](https://kptclsldc.in/WBUpload.aspx) **VF**. From the spreadsheet "details of IPPs under W n B as on 09.07.24.xlsx" (sheet 6, ESCOM-wise summary): **BESCOM 201 IPPs / 1,236.7 MW (111 old-WBA 763.7 MW + 90 GEOA 473.1 MW); GESCOM 177 / 2,004.5 MW; HESCOM 132 / 1,439.7 MW; MESCOM 21 / 89.2 MW; CESC 25 / 154.0 MW; TOTAL 556 IPPs / 4,924.1 MW (358 old-WBA 3,725.1 MW; 198 GEOA 1,199.0 MW)** — [file link via WBUpload.aspx](https://kptclsldc.in/WBUpload/details%20of%20IPPs%20under%20W%20n%20B%20as%20on%2009.07.24.xlsx) **VF**. DI: the backlog of ~360 "old WBA" generators (3.7 GW) with 10-year terms and renewals means legacy-rule settlement persists into the 2030s.
- From the consumer-list PDFs (text-extracted; GESCOM/HESCOM files failed to download, 1.2 KB error stubs): the BESCOM-jurisdiction list has **2,417 consumer-installation rows across 202 IPP IDs** (consumer ESCOM: BESCOM 2,031, CESC 147, GESCOM 125, HESCOM 77, MESCOM 37); CESC list 435 rows / 24 IPPs; MESCOM list 229 rows / 19 IPPs; **≈922 distinct consumer organisation names** after normalisation (my parse) — [BESCOM Consumer List.pdf](https://kptclsldc.in/WBUpload/BESCOM%20Consumer%20List.pdf) **VF (count) / EST (dedupe)**. Named consumers in these lists include Infosys Ltd, Wipro Ltd / Wipro GE Healthcare, Toyota Tsusho India, Shahi Exports (≥6 installations), Himatsingka Seide (Hassan), Kirloskar Ferrous (Koppal), Kalyani Steels (Ginigera), Mukand Ltd, Manipal Hospital, Narayana Hrudayalaya, Biocon, Sansera Engineering, ITC, Bangalore International Airport Ltd, AT&S India, Orient Cement, Dalmia Cement Bharat, JK Cement Works, Musashi Auto Parts, Federal-Mogul Goetze, Hitachi Astemo, 3M India, Manjushree Technopack, Cipla, Grindwell Norton, IFB Industries, Apotex Research, Jodhani Papers, Silver Crest Clothing, Ashirvad Pipes, MTR Foods, Jubilant Pharmova, Stelis Biopharma, Hetero Labs, Laurus Bio, Nestlé India, Schneider Electric, L&T, Reliance Jio, and IT-park owners Bagmane, RMZ, Manyata, Prestige, Brigade, Golflinks, Qualcomm, SAP Labs, NetApp **VF**. ## F2-Q2. Tamil Nadu (TNERC/TANGEDCO) and Maharashtra (MERC/MSEDCL) specifics

### Takeaway
Tamil Nadu notified Green Energy OA Regulations on 18 Sep 2025 (8% in-kind banking within the billing cycle, 14% / 12-month banking grandfathered for pre-April-2018 wind, unutilised banked energy paid at 75%, standby 125%, 63 kVA threshold), on top of two decades of litigated wind-banking rates (2%→5%→12%→14%) and an industry base of "1,500+ MSME textile mills" on OA/group-captive. Maharashtra remains the highest-charge large state (indicative CSS ₹1.50–2.20/unit, wheeling ₹0.18–1.70 by voltage) with active disputes (MERC-ordered refunds of wheeling charges) and a capacity-based banking proposal open for comment to Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
- TNERC Green Energy Open Access Regulations, 2025 (Notification TNERC/GEOA/25-1/2025, 18 Sep 2025): surplus offset within the billing cycle; **banking charge 8% in kind**; wind projects commissioned before 31 Mar 2018 keep 12-month banking at **14%**; unutilised banked energy settled by the DISCOM at **75% of the relevant tariff**; standby capped at **125%**; eligibility EHT/HT ≥63 kVA; TN SLDC nodal for short-term, STU for medium/long-term; metering at both ends per CEA; forecasting and DSM compliance mandatory; SLDC to issue energy-accounting procedures within 90 days; existing OA customers continue on existing agreements until expiry; concessions only for intra-state transactions — [SolarQuarter, 26 Sep 2025](https://solarquarter.com/2025/09/26/tnerc-issues-new-regulations-2025-for-green-energy-open-access-2025-in-tamil-nadu) **VF (secondary summary of regulation)**; corroborated by [Mercom snippet](https://mercomindia.com/tamil-nadu-notifies-green-energy-open-access-regulations-2025) **IC**. DI: TN settlement needs two banking rule-sets (legacy 12-month/14% and new monthly/8%) plus a 75% buy-back calculation.
- TN wind banking history: 2% (1986) → 5% (2001–2015) → 12% (2016 order) → 14% (2018 order, unchanged in 2020); 12-month banking in 2016, one-month without charge for post-Apr-2018 WEGs in 2018; TANGEDCO claims banking costs it ₹0.73/unit, generators claim a ₹2.18/unit gain to the DISCOM; APTEL (28 Jan 2021, Appeals 191/195/265 of 2018 & 406/2019) set aside the 2018 order as "half baked and wholly devoid of any logic" — [Prayas Energy Group, 17 Feb 2022](https://energy.prayaspune.org/power-perspectives/caught-in-a-whirlwind-a-commentary-on-competing-interests-and-regulatory-lacunae-in-the-provision-of-wind-energy-banking-in-tamil-nadu) **VF**. APTEL again overturned TNERC's wind banking-charge hike in June 2025 and ordered a re-study — [Mercom snippet](https://mercomindia.com/aptel-overturns-tamil-nadu-commissions-hike-in-wind-energy-banking-charges) **IC**. DI: banking rates in TN have been re-litigated at least 4 times; every reversal creates retroactive refund/true-up claims that a bill-audit engine can quantify.
- Industry bodies state "more than 1,500 MSME textile mills" in Tamil Nadu source OA power under group captive or own windmills; G.O. Ms. No. 37 (17 Apr 2018) directed TNERC to curtail OA for units <1 MW; the Jan 2026 G.O. amended the 2025 wind-repowering policy to restore banking/evacuation for older captive windmills — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sourcing/struggling-tamil-nadu-textile-industry-urges-not-change-states-energy-policies/) and [New Cloth Market](https://www.newclothmarketonline.com/?p=28835) **EST (association claims)**. DI: TN's long tail is MSME mills (<1 MW) with wind-captive legacy contracts: a low-ACV, high-count segment better served via associations (SIMA/TASMA) than direct sales.
- Maharashtra indicative FY2025-26 OA charges (vendor blog, labelled "indicative, ±5%", published 5 Jun 2026): wheeling **33 kV ₹0.18–0.25/unit (6% loss), 22/11 kV ₹0.85–1.05 (9% loss), LT ₹1.45–1.70 (12% loss)**; STU transmission ~₹0.40; CSS "typical" **₹1.50–2.20**, waived for group captive; banking **2–10% in kind**; additional surcharge not quantified; its own quick-summary (₹1.20–1.60) conflicts with its table — [Bridgeway Power blog](https://bridgewaypower.in/blog/solar-wheeling-charges-maharashtra-merc-2026) **EST**. Historical MERC additional surcharge ₹1.31/kWh (FY21) — Mercom snippet **IC**.
- MERC ordered MSEDCL to **refund unlawfully collected wheeling charges** and barred levying wheeling on OA power routed through the transmission network (Apr 2025), applying Case 206/2017 (Sridevi Trading v MSEDCL); further June 2025 orders in Cases 131/2024 and 120/121/153 of 2024 — [Energetica keyword page](https://www.energetica-india.net/keyword/open-access-charges) **VF**; [MERC order PDFs](https://merc.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Final-Order-in-Case-Nos.-131-of-2024.pdf) **VF (exists; not parsed)**. DI: direct evidence of DISCOM over-billing OA consumers in Maharashtra with regulator-ordered refunds: the core F2 value proposition has precedent.
- MERC has proposed a **capacity-based banking** framework for RE open access, comments due 12 Oct 2026 — [Mercom snippet](https://mercomindia.com/maharashtra-proposes-capacity-based-banking-for-renewable-open-access) **IC**. Gujarat capped GEOA banking at ₹1.50/unit (Aug 2026) — [SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2026/08/25/gerc-revises-green-energy-open-access-banking-charges-caps-rates-at-inr-1-50-per-unit-in-gujarat/) **VF (headline)**. Punjab draft: 15-min banking blocks, 10% charge — [SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2025/11/19/punjab-state-power-corporation-introduces-clear-banking-draft-procedure-to-support-green-energy-open-access-consumers-in-punjab/) **VF**. DI: banking is moving to 15-minute/capacity-based settlement in several states, which makes spreadsheet reconciliation infeasible and favours software.
- Delhi SLDC publishes monthly operating & transmission charge invoices for short-term OA (exchange-routed transactions excluded because exchanges pay on licensees' behalf) — [Delhi SLDC](https://delhisldc.org/) **VF**. Kerala: ~50 short-term OA consumers registered with SLDC and no 15-minute accounting existed for billing them — [KSEB order](https://kseb.in/uploads/Downloadtemsuppy/b-17149864122140522808.pdf) **VF (snippet)**.

### Inferences
- The three target states each run **two or more concurrent banking regimes** (legacy vs 2025 rules), with retroactive true-ups after APTEL/MERC rulings; the audit engine's first module should be "regime identification + refund entitlement" rather than tariff arithmetic alone [INF].
- Maharashtra is the highest-WTP state per unit (largest CSS/AS stack and documented refunds); Karnataka is the easiest to prospect (public SLDC consumer lists); Tamil Nadu has the largest count but smallest ACV [INF].

### Gaps
- No FY2025-26 MERC-notified MSEDCL ₹/unit schedule extracted (only vendor blog); TANGEDCO's current CSS/AS ₹/unit not found.
- No public count of OA consumers in TN or Maharashtra; TN SLDC OA registers not fetched.

## F2-Q3. Pricing anchors: bill audits, OA advisory, C&I energy-management SaaS

### Takeaway
No Indian consultant publishes an OA-advisory or electricity-bill-audit fee (searched; not found). Usable anchors are: BEE-style energy-audit price bands from a vendor guide (₹1.5–8 lakh per factory, ₹5–15 lakh for Designated Consumers, Apr 2026), Zenatix's historical 8–12%-of-energy-bill / ~₹30k per site/yr SaaS pricing (2017), JMK's ₹2 lakh OA market report, the CERC-capped ₹0.07/kWh intermediary trading margin, and the ₹0.40–1.65/kWh additional-surcharge swings that define the recoverable pool.

### Cited Findings
- Energy-audit indicative costs in India (vendor guide, updated Apr 2026): small office walkthrough ₹50k–1.5 lakh; mid-size factory Level-2 **₹1.5–4 lakh**; large industrial plant **₹3–8 lakh**; hospital/hotel ₹2–5 lakh; **BEE Designated Consumer Level 2–3 + compliance ₹5–15 lakh**; multi-site "volume pricing"; audit level keyed to annual energy spend (<₹25 lakh walkthrough; ₹25 lakh–2 Cr detailed; ₹2–10 Cr Level 2/3; >₹10 Cr investment-grade); "electricity bill/tariff audit" is not offered as a product — [Elion, 1 Apr 2026](https://elion.co.in/energy-audit-in-india-complete-guide-to-bee-compliance-process-cost-savings-2026/) **EST (vendor)**. DI: a ₹2–5 lakh/yr/site SaaS sits inside what plants already pay for a one-off audit.
- Zenatix (IoT energy management) "usually charges 8–12 per cent of the energy bill" with 10–30% savings, average revenue **~₹30,000 per site per year**, subscription with no upfront (Business Today, 4 Aug 2017) — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/magazine/features/story/the-power-people-84654-2017-08-04) **IC (dated)**; Zenatix now claims 3,000+ buildings and partners with Schneider — [Zenatix](https://www.zenatix.com/contact-us) **VF (vendor claim)**. No current pricing for Smart Joules or Ecolibrium found **UNK**.
- JMK sells its quarterly-updated "Open Access C&I RE Market in India" report at INR 2 lakh + GST — [JMK](https://jmkresearch.com/open-access-ci-re-market-in-india/) **VF** (from prior note).
- CERC (Petition 138/AT/2024) held that SECI's cumulative trading margin on BESS capacity resale must not exceed **₹0.07/kWh** (SECI had sought ₹0.07/kWh on top of 0.5% of capacity charge) — [CERC order](https://cercind.gov.in/2025/orders/138-AT-2024.pdf) **VF (snippet of order)**. DI: an intermediary take of ~₹0.05–0.07/kWh is the regulator-sanctioned reference for "handling" fees in Indian power transactions.
- IEX transaction fee ≈ ₹0.04/kWh traded ("industry reports"; IEX does not publish exactly) — [Tradejini blog](https://www.tradejini.com/blogs/iex-at-the-heart-of-indias-power-market-evolution) **EST**.
- Open Access Exchange claims clients see "15% to 40% reduction" in net energy cost; its pages list "end-to-end OA compliance & regulatory management", feasibility/landed-cost analysis, group-captive and third-party supply, but **no fee model and no named energy-accounting/banking-reconciliation service** — [Open Access Exchange](https://www.openaccessexchange.com/) **VF (vendor)**.
- Hero Future Energies case: advisory + connection costs paid back in <6 months on a ₹7.3 Cr/yr saving (amount not disclosed) — [HFE blog](https://www.herofutureenergies.com/blog/solar-open-access-for-commercial-industrial-consumers-india/) **EST** (prior note).
- Schneider Electric 2021 campaign offered free advisory to a limited number of facilities above a minimum bill threshold (developer/OEM-subsidised advisory pattern) — [Schneider T&C](https://go.schneider-electric.com/IN_202107-IOF-Media-Campaign_T-and-C_page.html) **VF**.

### Inferences
- A recoverable pool exists even for a modest site: at 6,230 MU/yr BESCOM OA drawal, a ₹0.10/kWh billing error rate (e.g., additional surcharge applied to captive drawal, wrong banking regime) is ₹62 Cr/yr across BESCOM alone; a 20–25% success fee on verified recoveries is the standard audit-firm structure and is consistent with the Zenatix "% of bill" precedent [INF/EST].
- Suggested price ladder from anchors: audit on success fee (20–25% of recovered amount, first 12–24 months), then ₹2–5 lakh/site/yr (≈ one detailed energy audit) for monthly settlement + procurement optimisation; enterprise multi-site at ₹15–40 lakh/yr (≈ DC-audit band) [INF].

### Gaps
- No published fee for OA advisory (Mercados, Idam, PManifold, CES, Elevate): searched, not found.
- No published price for Smart Joules, Ecolibrium, Enlog, Jouleflow.

## F3-Q2. Contract structures: merchant/co-optimisation allowed vs pure availability; FDRE structures and penalties; share by structure

### Takeaway
Central standalone BESS contracts via SECI have precedent for a **developer merchant tranche** (2022 tender: 60% SECI/40% developer third-party or market sale; pilot: 200 MW merchant portion arranged by the developer), while state DISCOM awards (KPTCL, TGGENCO, MSEDCL, NVVN, TNGECL) are capacity-charge contracts with cycle budgets and 95% availability obligations and no merchant provision stated. FDRE contracts (SECI FDRE-I to X) are energy-delivery contracts with **DFR 90% in peak hours / 80% off-peak / 90% annual, penalties of 1.5× tariff on shortfall** (FDRE-X cap 400 MWh per 100 MW), 25-year PPAs, and ≤5% market/bilateral sourcing. By awarded MW in 2025–26, availability-based standalone contracts dominate (estimated ~70–80%), FDRE/RTC ~20–30%, and merchant ≈0% of awards (but ~80% of operational GWh).

## F3-Q4. IEX participation and data-access terms

### Takeaway
A generator, CPP or OA consumer can bid on IEX directly as a **Proprietary Member** (net worth ₹150 lakh, 100%-cash security deposit with 3-year lock-in, SLDC consent for CPPs/OA consumers) or through a licensed **Trader Member**/**Facilitator Member**; fee schedules are "available on request"; 15-minute DAM/RTM price data from 1 Apr 2022 is downloadable without login under an "all rights reserved, quotation with attribution only" notice, so redistribution rights are unclear and must be contracted.

### Cited Findings
- IEX membership categories: Proprietary Member (grid-connected, trades own account, clears own contracts), Trader Member (licensed trader; own or client account), Facilitator Member (neither grid-connected nor licensed; acts for clients, cannot clear); Proprietary eligibility includes ISGS, state generators, IPPs, **CPPs and IPPs with SLDC consent**, distribution licensees, **OA consumers with SLDC consent**; net-worth requirement ₹150 lakh (electricity) for all three; fee and deposit structure "available on request from membership@iexindia.com" — [IEX Membership](https://www.iexindia.com/membership) **VF**. FAQs: initial security deposit 100% cash with 3-year lock-in; annual and client subscription fees run 12 months (Circular 64, 11 Aug 2011); trader clients must have a grid-connected entity for delivery — [IEX FAQs](https://iexindia.com/membership/faqs) **VF**; the fee-and-deposit page returned an error **UNK**.
- IEX DAM market snapshot: "Data in 15-minute time block format is available from delivery day 1st April 2022 onwards"; export button; no login prompt; disclaimer "All rights reserved. Only correct quotation with attribution is permitted", liability disclaimed — [IEX market snapshot](https://www.iexindia.com/market-data/day-ahead-market/market-snapshot) **VF**. DI: backtests on public 15-min data are feasible from day one; commercial redistribution of IEX data inside a product needs a licence conversation.
- 72% of cleared Integrated DAM volume came via members' bidding APIs (IEX Q1 FY27 slides) — prior note **IC**. IEX transaction fee ≈ ₹0.04/kWh (industry estimate) **EST**.
- Market design risks (coupling litigation, draft regs Apr 2026, peak-power contracts for BESS awaiting approval) — prior note **IC**.

### Inferences
- F3 need not be a trading member: the product can generate bid files/API calls executed by the customer's proprietary membership or a trader member (TPTCL, Manikaran etc.); the software vendor avoids ₹150 lakh net-worth and clearing obligations [INF].

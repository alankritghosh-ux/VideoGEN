## Q1. Could an incumbent bundle it? (H4: Big-4, TIC verifiers, ERP vendors, Indian/global carbon-accounting SaaS; H6: BEE-accredited verifiers, energy auditors, Big-4; H5: Mercom / Crisil-Bridge to India / JMK / Blackridge / GEM / CEA)

### Takeaway
H4's bundlers already hold both ends of the transaction: the EU importer (the only legally obligated party) gets the calculation and declaration from its ERP (SAP announced an end-to-end CBAM declarant solution on 25 Jun 2026 that "ingests supplier emissions data via Excel uploads"), and the exporter's actual data must be verified by an EU-accredited verifier whose Indian arms (TÜV India, SGS) already sell "pre-verification" and readiness work; at least one Indian SaaS (The Sustainability Cloud) already ships a "TSC CBAM Exporter" product. H6's verifiers are, so far, six small Indian energy-audit consultancies provisionally listed by BEE on 25 Mar 2026 (no Big-4, no TIC major) — weak bundlers, but they are exactly the firms the plant already pays for mandatory energy audits (₹3–15 lakh), and "prepare Form A" is a sub-task of that engagement. H5's records layer is already sold by four Indian vendors (Mercom tracker, Crisil-Bridge to India "India RE Navigator", JMK, Blackridge at ₹3,390/yr) and given away by GEM (1 MW+ operating, >20 MW prospective, with CEA plant-wise lists as a source).

## Q2. Is the apparent gap evidence of weak demand? (H4: declarant bears the obligation; defaults usable without verification; free-allocation phase-in; export volumes; H6: no CCC traded, no price, enforcement precedent; H5: do buyers pay for leads?)

### Takeaway
For H4 the law itself explains the gap: only the EU importer is obligated; it may declare on Commission default values with no verification at all ("The verification of embedded emissions should therefore only apply to actual values"), the Commission's own recital says declarants "should therefore be able to use default values in those first years, and rely on actual emissions subsequently", and the certificate obligation is scaled down by the EU-ETS free-allocation adjustment (Art. 31) that phases in over 2026–2034 — so the € that actual-data packs save in 2026–27 is a fraction of the headline €250/t numbers in vendor blogs. Indian finished-steel exports to the EU were front-loaded in FY2025-26 (Italy, Belgium, Spain = 34% of 6.6 Mt) and Argus expects flat-steel volumes to fall from July 2026 as EU quotas are halved. For H6, as of 7 Oct 2026 no CCC has traded, no floor/ceiling price exists, the environmental-compensation rule is keyed to "the average price at which carbon credit certificate is traded during the trading cycle" (undefined until trading), and the verifier bench in March 2026 was six provisional firms — there is no enforcement signal yet to sell compliance urgency against, and PAT's record (56% non-compliance, floor-price-only trades) is the precedent. For H5, buyers demonstrably pay for records-based trackers, but at ₹3,390–₹14,999 per seat per year, and nobody was found paying for satellite stage verification of Indian projects.

## Q5. Does the data have redistribution or legal risk?

### Takeaway

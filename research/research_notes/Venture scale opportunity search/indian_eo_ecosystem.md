## KQ1. Company-by-company: deployed product vs announcement, who pays, data owned vs licensed, pricing, funding, layer of stack

### Takeaway
India's EO "upstream" (Pixxel, GalaxEye, SatSure/KaleidEO, Dhruva) is well-funded but revenue-thin (no Indian space-tech startup crossed ₹100 Cr revenue in FY25; the biggest EO-analytics names are at ₹30–45 Cr with large losses), and the only EO-analytics businesses that are profitable in India are services-heavy, government-facing shops (Suhora, Vassar Labs). The one clearly repeatable *commercial* Indian buyer of satellite analytics is the agri-lending bank/NBFC (SatSure Sage; HDFC/ICICI/Kotak are both customers and shareholders). Pricing is not published by any Indian EO company.

### Inferences
- (HYP) The Indian EO-analytics stack has an empty "middle": upstream players are becoming data sellers (Pixxel, GalaxEye, KaleidEO) and the profitable downstream players are services shops selling to government. There is no Indian equivalent of a horizontal developer-facing data/API layer (Up42/SkyFi/Sentinel Hub) with Indian data and Indian public-records joins.
- (HYP) SatSure's moat shows the real asset is labelled ground truth + integration into bank workflows, built on free Sentinel-2 — a software-only founder can replicate the data side but not the 7-year bank relationships; adjacent, less-contested buyers (NBFC/MFI, FPO lenders, insurers' loss-assessment) remain.
- (HYP) Suhora/Vassar prove a bootstrapped ₹40–50 Cr profitable business is possible with government customers, but only with 60–190 staff and services economics, not venture-style software margins.

## KQ2. What the 2021 DST Geospatial Guidelines actually permit

### Takeaway
Since 15 Feb 2021 an Indian-owned startup needs **no prior approval, security clearance or licence** to acquire, process, store, publish or sell geospatial data/maps of any accuracy within India, subject only to a (still to-be-notified) negative list of sensitive attributes; data finer than 1 m horizontal / 3 m vertical must be owned by Indian entities and stored/processed on India-located servers, and foreign-controlled firms may only license (via API, not download) such fine data to serve Indian customers.

### Cited Findings
- Primary document: DST "Guidelines for acquiring and producing Geospatial Data and Geospatial Data Services including Maps", F.No. SM/25/02/2020, 15 Feb 2021. — [DST PDF](https://dst.gov.in/sites/default/files/Final%20Approved%20Guidelines%20on%20Geospatial%20Data.pdf) (2021-02-15; VF — but the PDF could not be fetched from this sandbox (503/DNS); provisions below are taken from three law-firm/legal-blog summaries that quote it). Informs: regulatory feasibility.
- Thresholds: on-site spatial accuracy 1 m horizontal (planimetry), 3 m vertical; gravity anomaly 1 milligal; bathymetry 10 m up to 500 m from shoreline, 100 m beyond. Negative-list attributes may carry different thresholds. — [SCC Online blog](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/03/04/india-geospatial-data-guidelines-2021/) (2021-03-04; IC); [Mondaq/JSA](https://www.mondaq.com/india/fiscal-monetary-policy/1053508/indias-revised-mapping-policy) (IC). Informs: 5 m Sentinel/Resourcesat and 0.3 m Cartosat-3 products are both legal to use; sub-1 m data must stay on Indian servers.
- No prior approval/licence: "no requirement for prior approval, security clearance, license or any other restrictions" on collection, generation, preparation, dissemination, storage, publication, updating, digitisation of geospatial data and maps in India; replaced by self-certification. — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/New-Guidelines-on-Liberalisation-of-Acquisition-and-Production-of-Geospatial-Data) (2021; IC); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/fiscal-monetary-policy/1053508/indias-revised-mapping-policy) (IC). Informs: no licensing cost/time for a startup.
- "Indian Entity" = Indian citizens, government entities, registered societies, statutory bodies, govt autonomous institutions, Indian companies/LLPs owned and controlled by resident Indian citizens (control per Rule 23, FEMA NDI Rules 2019). Only Indian Entities may: create/own data finer than threshold; access Indian ground stations/CORS/augmentation for real-time positioning and ground truthing; conduct terrestrial mobile mapping, street-view, and territorial-water surveys. — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/New-Guidelines-on-Liberalisation-of-Acquisition-and-Production-of-Geospatial-Data) (IC). Informs: a resident-Indian-founded startup is in the privileged class; **foreign VC control** could flip status — cap-table design matters.
- Foreign companies / foreign-controlled Indian companies: may license finer-than-threshold data from Indian Entities only to serve customers in India, and only via APIs that do not let the data pass through the licensee's servers; may not create/own such data. — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/New-Guidelines-on-Liberalisation-of-Acquisition-and-Production-of-Geospatial-Data) (IC); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/03/04/india-geospatial-data-guidelines-2021/) (IC). Informs: an Indian startup can be the compliant "data custodian" layer that foreign platforms must route through — a structural, policy-created role.
- Storage: data at/below threshold may be on any cloud; finer-than-threshold data must be stored and processed on domestic servers/cloud in India. — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/03/04/india-geospatial-data-guidelines-2021/) (IC). Informs: infra cost (Indian-region cloud).
- Negative list: DST to notify a list of "very sensitive attributes" with regulations; "there will not be any negative list of prohibited areas"; a Geospatial Data Promotion and Development Committee resolves disputes. Search did **not** find the notified list itself (status as of Oct 2026 unknown). — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/03/04/india-geospatial-data-guidelines-2021/) (IC); [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/New-Guidelines-on-Liberalisation-of-Acquisition-and-Production-of-Geospatial-Data) (IC). Informs: residual uncertainty on attributes like defence installations.
- Public-funded data: all geospatial data produced with public funds must be made easily accessible to all Indian Entities, without restrictions on use, except classified security/law-enforcement data; SoI and agencies told to drop forms/licences and use cloud/open APIs. — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/03/04/india-geospatial-data-guidelines-2021/) (IC); [Khaitan](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/New-Guidelines-on-Liberalisation-of-Acquisition-and-Production-of-Geospatial-Data) (IC). Informs: legal basis for demanding open government data (implementation lags, see KQ3/KQ5).
- Caveat: Khaitan notes the Guidelines do not expressly supersede the 2005 National Map Policy (Map Transaction Registry licences). — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/New-Guidelines-on-Liberalisation-of-Acquisition-and-Production-of-Geospatial-Data) (IC).

### Inferences
- (HYP) For a software-only Indian founder the regulatory environment is now a *non-issue* for building, and a mild *advantage* versus foreign platforms for sub-1 m data handling.

### Gaps

- Whether the negative-attribute list has been notified (2021–2026): not found.
- Status of the National Geospatial Policy 2022 implementation (NGDR/Bharat Map) was not researched here.

## KQ3. What ISRO/NRSC/Bhoonidhi/Bhuvan data is free or cheap, and real access terms

### Takeaway
Under Indian Space Policy 2023 as implemented on Bhoonidhi, **all IRS data of 5 m and coarser is free and open to everyone** (Resourcesat LISS-III/AWiFS, EOS-04 SAR ScanSAR/MRS, Oceansat, plus mirrored Sentinel-1/2 and Landsat-8/9) with a STAC-compliant API; **data finer than 5 m (Cartosat-1/2/2S/3 at 0.45–2.5 m, RISAT/EOS-04 stripmap, DEM <30 m) is free only to government entities and is sold to non-government entities by NSIL at prices that are not published online** ("contact eodata@nsilindia.co.in"). LISS-IV (5.8 m) status is not explicitly listed; it falls on the free side of the 5 m line by resolution but is not named in the open list.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- (HYP) For a software-only founder the usable free Indian stack is: Resourcesat-2/2A LISS-III (23.5 m) & AWiFS (56 m), EOS-04 C-band SAR ScanSAR/MRS ARD, EOS-06 OCM, plus mirrored Sentinel-1/2 — i.e. the same as the global open stack with the addition of Indian SAR. ## KQ4. Indian weather data: IMD, MOSDAC, NCMRWF, irradiance

### Takeaway
MOSDAC (ISRO) is free for registered users including commercial users (3-day latency for general users; NRT on case-by-case approval; cannot resell raw products but value-added products are unrestricted). IMD's observational data is **paid** through the Data Supply Portal (charges not published on the public page; estimate tool behind login; non-commercial undertaking prohibits commercial use) with only three aggregate series free. No public NCMRWF API or price could be verified (site 503). There is **no authoritative free satellite-derived irradiance product for India found**; VEDAS has a beta "Global Solar Calculator" and a 2021 NITI/ISRO energy map, and NIWE's SRRA ground network data is sold ("Wind/Solar Data Sale").

## KQ6. Evidence of Indian buyers paying for satellite analytics (tenders, values, winners)

### Takeaway
Verified paying demand clusters in three places: (1) private banks for agri-credit (SatSure's HDFC/ICICI/Kotak), (2) PMFBY YES-TECH, where states hire "Technology Implementation Partners" via least-cost tenders after central empanelment (minimum ₹5 Cr/yr turnover in similar work, ≥10 experts, ≥20 districts' experience; EMD ₹1 lakh) — but Madhya Pradesh chose a *state body* (MPCST) as TIP, and (3) defence/US government for upstream players. I found **no public award value for any satellite-analytics tender** in this scope; SECI, NHAI and Jal Shakti procurements found are ground-monitoring, drone-by-contractor or ISRO/BISAG-N MoUs rather than startup contracts.

### Cited Findings
- YES-TECH EoI (MNCFC, 18 Oct 2023): empanelment of Technology Implementation Partners for insurance-unit-level yield estimation "in all PMFBY implementing States/UTs from Kharif 2023 onwards"; sole bidders only (no consortia); open to government, private, international, autonomous agencies (international must have an Indian technical unit); eligibility: ≥10 experts on payroll (RS, GIS, stats, agri, data science), experience in tech-based yield estimation for ≥2 crops in ≥2 states, ≥20 districts across agro-climatic zones in 5 years, in-house lab, **minimum annual turnover ₹5 Cr in each of FY21–FY23 specifically from crop mapping/health/yield work**; EMD ₹1,00,000; technical score ≥60/100; empanelment 3 years; "Respective implementing States/UTs may issue a ToR to empanelled agencies for inviting financial bids… on the basis of the Least Cost System"; NDA with GoI; no advertising of empanelment. — [PMFBY EoI PDF](https://pmfby.gov.in/pdf/EoI_for_empanelment_of_WIP_under_YE_TECH_181023.pdf) (2023-10-18; VF — PDF text). Informs: **a new startup cannot qualify** (turnover/track-record gates); YES-TECH is L1-priced.

## KQ7. Where Indian EO-analytics startups have struggled (and why)

### Takeaway
The pattern across sources is consistent: Indian private demand expects free pilots and day-one reliability; government takes "up to two years from first meeting to revenue", selects on L1 and balance-sheet criteria that exclude startups, and prefers ISRO/legacy brands; the result is a sector where no company has ₹100 Cr revenue, the analytics leaders burn ₹30–40 Cr/yr, and the survivors either go upstream (satellites, with govt capital), go defence/US-government, or run lean profitable services shops. Grants/prize money appear as "latest rounds" even for Series-A/C companies (SatSure, Cropin, Suhora), and the first SAR demonstrator was lost.

### Cited Findings
- Revenue ceiling: among the 30 most valuable Indian space-tech startups none reached ₹100 Cr revenue in FY25; highest ₹73.02 Cr (XDLINX); "most others ₹1–5 crore". — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/magazine/why-indias-space-tech-start-ups-are-stuck-in-a-low-revenue-orbit) (2026-05-01/09-17; VF). 
- Willingness to pay: unnamed oil-company buyer asked for a free rather than paid pilot for a pipeline-leak/crop-disease product; Pixxel CEO: Indian firms expect solutions to "work from day one", want free pilots; "one or two" Indian customers vs "tens" in US; Indian corporate R&D ~0.7% of GDP vs 3–3.5% US. — same (VF quotes).
- Government cycle: "From first meeting to revenue can take up to two years in India"; L1 selection; eligibility (balance sheet, prior performance) excludes startups so "larger firms often win despite lacking core IP"; US gives initial responses in ~30 days, decisions in ~90. — same (VF).
- Domestic market thinness: "India is a small market for direct Earth observation customers except for the government"; only two customer types exist for most satellite builders (government and large telcos); government "often opts for legacy foreign brands". — [Outlook Business "Missing Piece"](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/start-up/deeptech/the-missing-piece-in-indias-space-tech-puzzle) (2024-09-01; VF).
- Pivot to defence: Digantara 80% govt/defence; GalaxEye expects 70:30 defence:commercial; Speciale Invest: "If they pick defence because it brings revenue, they have to accept that some trade-offs will follow." — [StartupPoint](https://startuppoint.in/indian-spacetech-startups-pivot-to-defence/) (2026-04-25; EST/IC with TechCrunch for Digantara).
- Domestic dependence: IN-SPACe data — Suhora >90% domestic, GalaxEye >90% domestic, MistEO 100% domestic, Digantara ~80% domestic, PierSight ~50% international; Pixxel CEO: "looking international from day one" is what they should have done better. — [Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/business/startups/how-indian-space-tech-startups-are-going-global-and-attracting-foreign-capital/2999531/1) (2026-10-16; VF as reported).
- Grant/prize dependence signals: Tracxn lists "Grant (prize money)" as the *latest* round for SatSure ($2.6 Mn, Jun 2026), Cropin (~$0.8 Mn, Aug 2025), Suhora (Jun 2026, IN-SPACe); GalaxEye's post-failure capital is a ₹63.84 Cr TDB/RDI-Fund convertible debenture; Satyukt's backers include BIRAC and C-CAMP (public). — Tracxn MCP (EST); [KnowStartup](https://knowstartup.com/news/galaxeye-rs-64-crore-govt-funding-optosar-satellite) (IC). Informs: even scaled players lean on public money.
- Attrition/dormancy: Blue Sky Analytics at ₹3.9 lakh FY25 revenue, 8 staff, website unresolvable (Tracxn; EST). Cosmoserve pivoted from advanced debris systems to large-debris removal because "customers only paid for that" (Outlook; VF). GalaxEye lost its first satellite two months after launch (ThePrint, Jul 2026; IC).
- Burn vs revenue (FY25, Tracxn/Inc42): SatSure ₹31.5 Cr rev / ₹42.6 Cr loss; Cropin ₹42.8 Cr / ₹30.5 Cr loss; Pixxel (India) ₹39.3 Cr / ₹30.8 Cr loss; Dvara ₹16.7 Cr / ₹54.6 Cr loss; GalaxEye ₹2.0 Cr / ₹9.6 Cr loss. Profitable: Suhora ₹41.4 Cr / +₹1.5 Cr; Vassar ₹46.7 Cr / +₹2.3 Cr; Skymet FY24 +₹1.3 Cr (FY25 –₹3.6 Cr). (EST — database figures derived from MCA filings.)
- YES-TECH backlash: MP soybean farmers demanded rollback citing lower claims; a Bhaskar report headlined "₹1 lakh crop insured for just ₹2,400; reason—satellite survey far from reality"; TIP admits 20–30% error room. — [CSE MP report](https://www.cseindia.org/content/downloadreports/13110) (2026-05-18; VF). Informs: reputational/political risk of being the yield-estimation vendor.
- Launch access bottleneck (upstream only): 4–8 Indian launches/yr vs 100+ US; a rocket startup waited >1 year for a slot. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/magazine/why-indias-space-tech-start-ups-are-stuck-in-a-low-revenue-orbit) (VF). Not relevant to software-only.

### Inferences
- (HYP) The failure mode for a software-only EO startup in India is not technology or data access (both are now adequate and mostly free) but *customer selection*: targeting government or large corporates leads to 1–2-year cycles and free-pilot expectations. The evidence points to buyers with a direct, regulated, recurring money consequence (lenders, insurers, EUDR-exposed exporters) as the only ones with demonstrated willingness to pay.
- (HYP) A durable, capital-light position is a developer/analyst-facing data layer (open Indian EO + public records + parcel geometry) sold to the ~10–20 Indian analytics shops, banks' in-house teams and global platforms that need a compliant Indian data custodian under the 2021 guidelines — adjacent to, rather than competing with, SatSure/Cropin.

### Gaps
- No systematic dataset of pilot-to-contract conversion rates or sales-cycle lengths exists; evidence is anecdotal (founder quotes).
- No confirmed shutdown list for Indian EO-analytics startups 2024–26 beyond dormancy signals.

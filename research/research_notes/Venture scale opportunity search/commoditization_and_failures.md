Hostile, evidence-first notes. All sources accessed 2026-10-17. Evidence classes used throughout:

"Decision informed" tags: *(D: moat test)* = feeds the eight-hypothesis table; *(D: build/no-build)* = feeds the ranked reasons-not-to-build list.

## Key Question 1 — EO analytics graveyard and survivors: what happened to analytics-on-top-of-imagery as a standalone business?

### Takeaway
Every venture-backed, horizontal "analytics on third-party imagery" company examined either died, was fire-sold, or pivoted to government/defence; the survivors in EO are the companies that *own the sensors* (Planet, BlackSky, Satellogic, Vantor) and sell mostly to governments, and even they have struggled to make commercial analytics a material revenue line. The record is close to unanimous.

### Cited Findings

**Orbital Insight (≈$130M raised; GV, In-Q-Tel backed) — fire sale to Privateer, 2024**
- Investigative report (Jack Poulson, Substack, pub. 2023-10-01): Orbital Insight raised $130M; cut its liquidation preference stack from $200M → $137M → $50M in 2023 to raise emergency bridge money; estimated 2023 revenue ≈$9.5M of which ≈$5M expected from the Indonesian military; sued for default on $370k/month HQ rent; headcount cut to ~40, entire sales and marketing team laid off; commercial business "on ice"; a $950M-ceiling JADC2 contract had only $1,800 in recorded payouts; failed sale approaches to Descartes Labs, Ursa Space, Janes, Ankura, Planet Labs and Scale AI before Privateer. — [Poulson, "Exclusive: Google-backed intelligence contractor is avoiding bankruptcy..."](https://jackpoulson.substack.com/p/exclusive-google-backed-intelligence) — **EST → IC** (single investigative source for the financial detail; the Privateer deal itself was later confirmed by Reuters/Via Satellite, so the distress narrative is corroborated in outcome). *(D: build/no-build, moat test: "proprietary data", "validated prediction records")*
- Privateer (Steve Wozniak co-founded) announced acquisition of Orbital Insight 2024-05-06 alongside a $56.5M Series A; price undisclosed; TerraScope analytics platform to be folded into Privateer's Wayfinder. — [Via Satellite, 2024-05-08](https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2024/05/08/steve-wozniaks-privateer-acquires-orbital-insight/); [Reuters via Inc.](https://inc.com/reuters/wozniaks-space-firm-privateer-buys-orbital-insight-raises-565-million.html) — **IC**.

Interpretation: the *data/analytics* layer (AIS-derived maritime intelligence) was worth more to a vertical data incumbent (Kpler, commodities intelligence) than to the satellite operator — analytics got absorbed as a feature of a distribution-owning incumbent. *(D: moat test "distribution", "transaction access")*

**Sector synthesis (secondary, opinion-grade but consistent with the primary data above)**
- "More commercial satellite imagery capacity than paying customers"; government customers are a disproportionate share of revenue; commercial adoption (Deere, energy, finance) slower than pre-SPAC projections; ESA's free Sentinel-2 caps pricing at the low end; "the bottleneck is analytics, not collection"; predicted consolidation to a few survivors. — [New Space Economy, 2026-04-08](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/04/08/the-satellite-imagery-glut-too-much-data-too-few-paying-customers-and-a-coming-shakeout-in-earth-observation/) — **HYP**.
- Maxar taken private by Advent for $6.4B (2023), split into Vantor (imagery/analytics) and Lanteris (manufacturing) in Oct 2025; Vantor serves >400,000 US-government users via NGA's G-EGD. — same source — **EST**.

### Inferences
- No horizontal "insights-from-other-people's-pixels" company has reached durable scale: Orbital Insight (~$130M raised → distressed sale), Descartes (~$100M → fire sale), Astraea (dead), Hypergiant (PE bolt-on), Gro (dead, see KQ4). The pattern is identical across 2020–2024, i.e., *before* frontier-model pressure — so the failure mode is structural (small commercial ACVs, long sales cycles, government dependence), not just AI commoditization.
- Companies that survived own the sensor (vertical integration) **and** sell to sovereigns. Even for them, "commercial" is 18% (Planet) and shrinking US-federal share (BlackSky) is being backfilled by non-US governments, not by enterprises.
- Analytics units get absorbed as features by whoever owns distribution to the end buyer (Kpler buying Spire Maritime; Privateer buying Orbital; Trive/Forward Slope buying Hypergiant). This is the strongest recurring signal in the EO record.

### Gaps
- No disclosed price for Descartes Labs or Orbital Insight; "fraction of prior valuation" is a reasonable inference from the $50M preference-stack cut (Poulson) but not a verified deal price.
- RS Metrics, SpaceKnow, Ursa Space: no revenue, layoff or valuation data found (search budget exhausted).
- Planet's exact FY2026 segment table was not reached in the 10-K fetch; the 59/23/18 split rests on three concordant secondary write-ups.
- Kpler/Spire: UK CMA final decision not confirmed.

## Key Question 2 — Weather/forecasting: do open AI weather models compress the price of commercial forecasting, and who has failed/consolidated?

### Takeaway
The global forecasting *model* is now open and essentially free (ECMWF AIFS operational and open; Google WeatherNext outputs in BigQuery/Earth Engine with CC-BY-4.0 historical data; Open-Meteo CC-BY data on 30+ models), so the pure "better global model" business has no price floor. Direct evidence of vendor price compression is thin (vendors do not publish prices), but the failure/consolidation list in adjacent "climate intelligence" is long (ClimateAi Aug 2026, Gro 2024, Cervest absorbed into Mitiga, Solcast absorbed into DNV), and survivors are either sensor-owners (Tomorrow.io) or incumbents with distribution (DTN, Vaisala, DNV).

### Cited Findings

**Open / free model outputs**
- ECMWF AIFS Single became operational 2025-02-25 — "the first fully operational weather prediction open model", openly available with 24/7 support; outperforms physics-based models on many measures incl. tropical-cyclone tracks "with gains of up to 20%"; ~1,000× less energy per forecast; 28 km grid (vs 9 km IFS). — [ECMWF news, 2025-02-25](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2025/ecmwfs-ai-forecasts-become-operational) — **VF**. AIFS ENS (ensemble) operational June 2025 per ECMWF slides (March 2026). — [ECMWF/EUSPA slides](https://eu-space.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/2_AIRevolution_EarthSystem_Chantry_March2026.pdf) — **EST** (slide deck, not fetched in full). AIFS paper: ~10% improvement over IFS through the troposphere. — [arXiv 2406.01465](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.01465) — **VF (abstract-level)**. *(D: build/no-build)*
- Google WeatherNext: WeatherNext 3 (Aug 2026) is flagship; WeatherNext 2 (June 2025), WN1 Gen (Dec 2024), WN1 Graph (Nov 2023) remain available; delivered via BigQuery, Earth Engine, Cloud Storage (allow-list); **historical data (≥1 h old) is CC BY 4.0**; real-time/future forecasts under "GDM Real-Time Weather Forecasting Experimental Data Terms"; 0.05°–0.25° resolution, 15-day lead, 64 ensemble members initialised hourly; claims "up to 50% reduction in Brier score and CRPS" vs NWP baselines on precipitation; no published price. — [Google WeatherNext developer guide](https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/models) — **VF**. Vertex AI custom inference was "early access" at WN2 launch (Nov 2025) with pricing undisclosed. — [9to5Google, 2025-11-17](https://9to5google.com/2025/11/17/google-weathernext-2/); [DeepMind WeatherNext](https://deepmind.google/science/weathernext/) — **IC**.
- Open-Meteo: free tier (non-commercial) 10,000 calls/day, 300,000/month; >30 national models (ECMWF, DWD, NOAA, Météo-France, JMA, UKMO…) at 1–11 km; **server code AGPLv3, data CC BY 4.0**; paid commercial tiers at 1M/5M/50M+ calls per month, prices not published; third parties note you can self-host instead of paying. — [Open-Meteo pricing](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing) — **VF**. *(D: moat test "proprietary data")*

**Commercial vendor economics (thin)**
- Tomorrow.io: 2021 SPAC deck projected $11M (2021) → $389M (2025) → $747M (2026) revenue; the SPAC was abandoned; no audited revenue found. Best-documented revenue is government: $19.3M USAF contract for weather radar sats; two microwave sounder sats for USAF (2024); $2.27M NOAA NESDIS (2024); DoD total ≈$20M (Wikipedia); 13-satellite constellation completed by Feb 2026. — [Via Satellite, 2021-12-07](https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2021/12/07/tomorrow-io-to-go-public-via-spac-to-fund-satellite-constellation); [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/tomrorow-io-wins-air-force-contract/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomorrow.io) — **EST**. Inference: the "weather intelligence SaaS" story has been replaced by a sensor + government story. *(D: build/no-build)*
- Meteomatics: no public price list; custom usage-based packages; G2 feedback (as summarised by an auto-generated comparison site) notes "limited pricing flexibility and surprise versus low-cost or open-source weather APIs". — [rfp.wiki comparison](https://www.rfp.wiki/specialty-industries/energy-utilities-software/weather-data-solutions-for-energy-and-utilities/meteomatics/tomorrow-io) — **HYP** (low-quality source).
- Solcast → DNV, announced 2023-02-01, terms undisclosed, kept as a unit under existing management in Australia; founder said DNV's interest "made my decision to sell so much easier". — [DNV release](https://www.dnv.com/news/2023/dnv-expands-solar-forecasting-expertise-with-the-acquisition-of-solar-data-specialist-solcast-238386/); [Solcast blog](https://solcast.com/blog/why-did-solcast-choose-dnv) — **VF** (terms UNK). Solcast's method: physical downscaling + bias correction with real-time SCADA harvested via API to auto-update bias terms. — [Solcast Premium Power](https://solcast.com/premium-pv-wind-power-models) — **VF**. *(D: moat test "customer-specific calibration")*

**Failures / consolidation in climate & weather intelligence**
- **ClimateAi** (SF; ~$38M raised; Footprint Coalition-backed; customers Dole, Suntory, Oatly, McCain) wound down and returned capital, announced on LinkedIn ~2026-08-07; CEO cited "geopolitical and climate headwinds"; had launched an "agentic AI" harvest-window tool in Jan 2026 — i.e., adding agents did not save it. — [AgFunderNews, 2026-08-08](https://agfundernews.com/climate-resilience-platform-climateai-winds-down-operations); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/144261-climateai-shuts-down-after-raising-38m-in-funding/) — **IC**. *(D: build/no-build)*
- **Cervest** (London; $36.2M raised incl. $30M Series A 2021): cervest.earth now 301-redirects to earth-scan.com, which is branded "EarthScan by Mitiga Solutions" with no mention of Cervest; Craft.co lists Cervest as "Closed". — [earth-scan.com](https://www.earth-scan.com/); [Craft](https://craft.co/cervest); [TechCrunch Series A, 2021](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2155051) — **IC** that the independent company no longer exists; the mechanism (asset sale vs. wind-down) is **UNK**. The task brief's "Cervest shutdown 2024/25" is therefore *partly* verified: the brand is gone, the product survives inside another vendor.
- **Jupiter Intelligence**: still operating in 2025–26 (Arcadis partnership 2025, ClimateScore upgrades July 2025, PE clients); $23M Series B led by Energize. No layoffs/acquisition found. — [Arcadis, 2025](https://www.arcadis.com/en/news/global/2025/arcadis-and-jupiter-intelligence-partner-to-accelerate-global-climate-resilience-solutions); [Fintech Global, 2025-07-23](https://fintech.global/2025/07/23/jupiter-sets-new-standard-for-climate-risk-analytics/) — **IC** (alive) / **UNK** (financials). Note the go-to-market: distribution via an engineering consultancy (Arcadis) rather than direct SaaS.
- **One Concern, Climate X**: no 2025–26 status information found (search budget exhausted) — **UNK**.
- No source found that quantifies forecasting-vendor price declines attributable to AI models — **UNK**. The compression argument rests on the licensing facts above (open outputs, CC-BY, free tiers) rather than on observed vendor price cuts.

### Inferences
- The global-model layer has been commoditised by public and hyperscaler actors (ECMWF, Google, national met services via Open-Meteo). Any startup whose value proposition is "a better global forecast" is competing with free, with incumbents' distribution and with Google's TPU fleet.
- What has *not* been commoditised: (a) proprietary observations (Tomorrow.io's radar sats, Vaisala/DTN sensor networks), (b) asset-specific calibration using customer SCADA (Solcast, Meteologica — see KQ7), (c) distribution/accountability wrappers (DNV, Arcadis, DTN). Note that (b) and (c) are exactly where the startups got *acquired* rather than where they stayed independent.
- "Climate intelligence" for food/ag enterprises has now produced two clean failures (Gro 2024, ClimateAi 2026) and one disappearance (Cervest); the buyers (CPG, agribusiness) demonstrably did not pay enough.

### Gaps
- Solcast–DNV price; Tomorrow.io audited revenue; Meteomatics/DTN/Vaisala pricing trends; One Concern and Climate X status; whether Google charges for real-time WeatherNext via Vertex.
- Microsoft Aurora (Nature 2025) and NVIDIA Earth-2: paywall redirect / search budget; licence terms not verified — **UNK**.

## Key Question 3 — Energy analytics: do forecasting/analytics companies get absorbed as features by incumbents?

### Takeaway
Yes, with a 2-to-3-year half-life: AutoGrid went Schneider (2022) → Uplight (Dec 2023) in 19 months; Solcast → DNV; Spire Maritime → Kpler; Hypergiant → PE roll-up. Stem — the poster child of "software-defined storage" — wrote off $547M of goodwill, did a 1-for-20 reverse split and cut 27% of staff while pivoting *to* software, a reminder that "pivoting to the analytics layer" is what distressed hardware companies do, not a safe harbour. Independent energy-data startups that remain (Amperon, GridStatus) are small (~$30M total raised for Amperon).

### Cited Findings
- **AutoGrid**: Schneider Electric agreed to acquire AutoGrid in May 2022; agreed to divest it to Uplight 2023-12-14 (~19 months later), terms undisclosed; Schneider is also a strategic investor in Uplight (led 2021 round at $1.5B valuation); combined entity ~700 staff, 8,300 MW flexible capacity. — [Uplight release, 2023-12-14](https://uplight.com/press/uplight-to-acquire-autogrid/); [Memoori, 2023-12-18](https://memoori.com/behind-schneider-electric-move-divest-autogrid-uplight/); [BizWest](https://bizwest.com/?p=373261) — **VF/IC**. *(D: build/no-build)*
- **Stem Inc.** FY2025 10-K: revenue $156.3M (FY24 $144.6M); hardware still 43.9% of revenue ($68.6M) vs 53.1% ($76.8M) in FY24; **goodwill impairment $547.2M in 2024**; FY2024 net loss $854.0M; FY2025 "net income" $137.8M (driven by debt exchanges — my reading, not stated); 1-for-20 reverse split effective 2025-06-23 after NYSE sub-$1 notice (2024-08-28); ~27% workforce reduction announced 2025-04-09 ($6.1M restructuring); June 2025 exchanges of ~$228.8M of 2028 notes and ~$121.3M of 2030 notes into $155.4M new 2030 senior secured notes; cash $48.9M at 2025-12-31; non-affiliate market cap $50.0M at 2025-06-30; explicit strategy to move from "battery resales" to PowerTrack software/services; "may continue to incur net losses through at least 2026". — [Stem 10-K FY2025, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1758766/000175876626000016/stem-20251231.htm) — **VF**. No going-concern paragraph in the portions read; MD&A states 12-month sufficiency. *(D: build/no-build)*
- **Enverus roll-up**: acquired Energy Acuity (Mar 2021, power-generation market data) and RatedPower (2022-09-14, Madrid, solar plant design automation), both terms undisclosed. — [Enverus newsroom](https://www.enverus.com/newsroom/enverus-rises-to-meet-solar-planning-challenges-with-acquisition-of-ratedpower/); [PV Tech](https://pv-tech.org/pv-software-provider-ratedpower-acquired-by-enverus) — **VF**. Pearl Street: **UNK** (search budget; Enverus newsroom returned 403).
- **Amperon** (demand forecasting): $20M Series B led by Energize (D. E. Shaw, Veriten, HSBC AM); total raised ≈$30–31M; strategic money from National Grid Partners (Mar 2025) and Tokyo Gas/Acario (Jul 2025); stated plan to move from demand forecasting to "holistic data analytics". — [Kurrant, 2025-07-22](https://kurrant.com/kurrantly-news/tokyo-gas-venture-arm-backs-ai-energy-forecasting-firm-amperon/); [Clay dossier](https://www.clay.com/dossier/amperon-funding) — **IC/EST**. Note: utility CVCs as investors = customers who may prefer to own the capability.
- **Yes Energy**: last recorded acquisition Power Settlements (2024-04-23). — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/yes-energy/financials) — **EST**. GridStatus pricing page returned 403 — **UNK**.
- **Grid-operator vendor behaviour** (relevant to "features vs vendors"): ERCOT's operating procedures tell operators *not* to toggle between two wind-forecast vendors for marginal accuracy ("chasing the forecast"); a switch requires sustained, logged under-performance (e.g., forecast > actual HSL by >2,000 MW for ≥3 h). — [ERCOT Power Operations Bulletin 1054](https://www.ercot.com/files/pobs/2022/08/31/Power_Operations_Bulletin_1054.doc) — **VF**. Cuts both ways: incumbency is sticky, but ERCOT runs *multiple* vendors in parallel, which caps any one vendor's pricing power.
- **Bidgely, Opus One (GE Digital), Enbala (Generac), Fluence IQ**: not reached — **UNK**.

### Inferences
- Energy analytics startups are acquired at small, undisclosed prices by whoever owns the utility/IPP relationship (Schneider, Uplight, DNV, Enverus). Undisclosed terms across *every* deal found is itself a signal: material prices get announced.
- Strategic investors (National Grid, Tokyo Gas, Schneider) are both the exit route and the ceiling; they buy the feature once it is de-risked.
- Stem shows that "software/services" is where distressed hardware companies retreat *to*, so the analytics layer in energy is crowded with motivated, well-funded incumbents, not empty.

### Gaps
- Deal prices for AutoGrid, Solcast, RatedPower, Energy Acuity, Hypergiant, Orbital Insight, Descartes: all undisclosed.
- Churn/retention data for any energy-analytics vendor: none found.

## Key Question 4 — Vertical data moats: when did a dataset or validated track record create pricing power, and what distinguishes survivors?

### Takeaway
Survivors (Verisk, CoreLogic/Cotality, Enverus, Kpler, DNV) own *contributory or transaction-exhaust data* plus the buyer relationship, and are mostly decades-old incumbents or PE roll-ups. The venture-backed "we'll build the dataset from public/third-party feeds and sell insight" cohort — Gro (dead), Farmers Edge (IPO C$17 → taken private at C$0.35), Indigo ($3.5B → reported ~$200M), Climate Corp (Bayer cut FieldView from $350/yr to $49/yr), Zillow Offers ($500M+ write-downs when its own prediction record met real transactions) — did not get pricing power from data.

Startups that *assembled* data from third parties (Gro, Orbital, Descartes) had none of the three.
- A "validated prediction record" is necessary but demonstrably insufficient: Zillow had the most public record in its category and lost ~$550M when it bore transaction risk on it; forecast accuracy did not translate to pricing power for Orbital/Descartes either.
- Price trajectories in ag-data (FieldView $350 → $49) show that when the data owner is also the product seller, analytics gets priced at ~zero as a retention feature — bad news for any independent analytics vendor in that vertical.

## Key Question 5 — Frontier-model effects 2024–2026: which EO/weather/time-series capabilities are now "free" and which still need proprietary data?

### Takeaway
Three layers are now free or near-free: global medium-range weather forecasts (AIFS, WeatherNext, Open-Meteo), general-purpose EO embeddings (AlphaEarth Foundations in Earth Engine; Clay, Prithvi open weights), and zero-shot time-series forecasting (Chronos-2, TimesFM-2.5, Moirai-2 beat statistical baselines on GIFT-Eval). What remains non-free is *labels and ground truth tied to a specific asset or customer* — the foundation models explicitly market themselves as needing fewer labels, not zero.

### Cited Findings
- **AlphaEarth Foundations** (Google DeepMind, 2025-07-30): 10 m × 10 m annual embeddings for all land and coastal waters, released as the "Satellite Embedding" dataset in Google Earth Engine; "24% lower error rate" than the models tested across 15 mapping tasks, "particularly when label data was scarce"; 16× less storage; >50 organisations testing. Baselines in the paper included Prithvi, Clay, SatCLIP and CCDC. — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphaearth-foundations-helps-map-our-planet-in-unprecedented-detail/); [arXiv 2507.22291 summary](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/alphaearth-foundations-an-embedding) — **VF** (self-reported benchmark; no independent head-to-head found). *(D: build/no-build)*
- Clay v1.5 and IBM/NASA Prithvi are open encoders used without fine-tuning in independent 2026 research; a secondary flood-mapping comparison puts Clay v1.5 at 0.79 mIoU (PlanetScope) / 0.70 (Sentinel-2) with Prithvi leading on Sentinel-1. — [arXiv 2604.03456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03456); [Medium (unverified)](https://medium.com/@brian-curry-research/geospatial-foundation-models-for-agricultural-machine-learning-a3cd0169d256) — **EST/HYP**.
- **Weather**: AIFS Single operational and open (Feb 2025), up to 20% better on TC tracks, ~1,000× less energy; WeatherNext 3 (Aug 2026) with hourly 64-member ensembles and CC-BY-4.0 historical outputs; GenCast claimed to beat ECMWF ENS. — [ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2025/ecmwfs-ai-forecasts-become-operational); [Google WeatherNext guide](https://developers.google.com/weathernext/guides/models); [WeatherBench 2 FAQ](https://sites.research.google/weatherbench/faq/) — **VF**. Caveat: early AIFS lagged IFS on *surface* variables at 1° (ECMWF Newsletter #178) — the surface/asset layer is where residual value sits. — [ECMWF Newsletter](https://www.ecmwf.int/node/28950) — **VF (older)**.
- **Time series**: Chronos-2 (Amazon, arXiv 2510.15821, Oct 2025) reports GIFT-Eval win rate 79.8% / skill 46.6% (WQL) vs TimesFM-2.5 70.0%, Moirai-2.0 56.0%, Chronos-Bolt 49.4%, **Seasonal Naive 10.1%**; zero-shot incl. covariates; authors disclose partial pretraining overlap with GIFT-Eval splits. — [arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15821) — **VF (self-reported)**. GIFT-Eval paper (Salesforce): foundation models generally beat statistical and deep-learning baselines across domains but "struggle on high-variability domains such as transportation and Web/Cloud"; scaling benefits held in some domains incl. **energy**. — [Salesforce blog](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/gift-eval-time-series-benchmark/) — **VF**. *(D: build/no-build)*
- WeatherNext Vertex AI pricing undisclosed; AlphaEarth licence not stated on the blog — **UNK**.

### Inferences
- A solo founder can today, at zero marginal data cost, run: global 15-day ensemble weather (CC-BY), 10 m planetary embeddings (Earth Engine), and SOTA zero-shot demand/load forecasting (open weights). Everything that used to be a Series-A-funded "platform" in 2018–2021 is a notebook.
- What the frontier models do *not* supply: asset-level ground truth (SCADA, meter data, yield, claims), local observations (radar, station networks), and the legal right to use a customer's data. That is where the remaining moat sits — and it belongs to the customer, not to the vendor, unless contractually captured.
- Benchmarks are self-reported by the model owners (Google, Amazon, ECMWF). Treat the magnitude as EST even where the direction is uncontested.

### Gaps
- No independent 2026 head-to-head of AlphaEarth vs Clay vs Prithvi on the same tasks.
- WeatherBench 2 numbers for AIFS ENS vs GenCast vs WeatherNext 3 not retrieved.
- Microsoft Aurora and NVIDIA Earth-2 licence/commercial terms — UNK.

## Key Question 6 — Software deflation: credible measurements of AI coding productivity and SaaS pricing pressure 2025–2026

### Takeaway
The only randomised evidence (METR) shows a 19% *slowdown* in early 2025 and point estimates of 4–18% *speed-up* by late 2025 with confidence intervals that include zero — i.e., real but unproven and far below "10×" claims. Market evidence of SaaS compression is loud but mostly secondary: a Feb–Mar 2026 software sell-off, Atlassian −57–69% YTD and 1,600 layoffs, Airtable sold for $1.285B (≈2.7× ARR, vs $11.7B peak), and outcome-pricing pilots (Intercom Fin $0.99/resolution). Direct evidence that agents reduce *vertical* SaaS defensibility is thin and contested.

### Cited Findings
- **METR RCT (early 2025)**: 16 experienced OSS developers, 246 tasks; developers forecast +24% speed-up; measured **19% slower** (95% CI +2% to +39%); they still believed they were ~20% faster afterwards. — [TechCrunch, 2025-07-11](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/11/ai-coding-tools-may-not-speed-up-every-developer-study-shows) — **IC**.
- **METR update (2026-02-24)**: 57 developers (median 10 yrs experience), 143 repos, 800+ tasks, pay cut to $50/h; original-cohort subset −18% time (CI −38% to +9%); new recruits −4% (CI −15% to +9%); 30–50% of developers withheld tasks they did not want to do without AI; METR calls its own data "very weak evidence" for the size of any speed-up and is redesigning the study. — [METR blog, 2026-02-24](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) — **VF**. *(D: build/no-build — software cost deflation is real but the measured magnitude is modest)*
- **DORA 2025**: frames AI as "an amplifier, magnifying an organization's existing strengths and weaknesses"; quantitative throughput/instability figures not retrieved. — [DORA 2025](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/) — **VF (qualitative only)**.
- **Software sell-off**: multiple secondary sources describe ~$285B of software market value lost in Feb 2026 (one claims ~$1T in the first week of February) on fears that agents cut seat counts; HubSpot −51%, Monday −44%, ServiceNow −36%, Salesforce −28%, Adobe −25% are cited by low-quality aggregators and conflict on details. — [Kavout](https://www.kavout.com/market-lens/is-the-saaspocalypse-truly-over); [Minster Community Post / MarketMinute](https://business.minstercommunitypost.com/minstercommunitypost/article/marketminute-2026-2-16-the-saaspocalypse-of-2026-why-applovin-and-ciscos-record-earnings-are-falling-on-deaf-ears) — **EST** (direction corroborated, magnitudes unverified).

Claim of "first-ever decline in enterprise seat counts" comes from a single blog and is **UNK**.
- **Airtable → Bending Spoons**, announced 2026-08-04, $1.285B cash for the core SaaS (≈2.7× ~$480M ARR) vs $11.7B Dec-2021 Series F; agent unit carved out. — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/blogs/airtable-the-saaspocalypse-and-a-hail-mary) — **EST** (single blog; price unsourced). The same blog cites Intercom Fin at $0.99/resolution and HubSpot at $0.50/resolved conversation and $1/qualified lead (Apr 2026) as outcome pricing — **EST**.
- Bain (as quoted): per-seat-only pricing fell from 21% to 15% of SaaS vendors over twelve months; IDC: 70% of vendors to refactor pricing by 2028. — [Kavout](https://www.kavout.com/market-lens/is-the-saaspocalypse-truly-over) — **EST** (not traced to primary).
- **Vertical AI defensibility (opinion)**: Bessemer's Jan 2026 playbook ranks workflow position as the moat (core workflows = deeper moat; tertiary workflows = safer wedge) and emphasises sector data + regulatory complexity; a16z's earlier essays argue data alone is rarely a sufficient moat; Forgent argues general agents with native connectors (Drive, M365) thin out data-access moats; Orion VC argues moats are moving to RL environments/evals. — [Forgent](https://www.forgent.ai/blog/defensibility-in-the-age-of-ai-2026); [Orion VC](https://orionvc.substack.com/p/2025-in-review); [SignalFire](https://signalfire.com/blog/why-ai-startups-should-optimize-for-permanence-not-moats) — **HYP**. ### Inferences
- "Software is nearly free" is directionally supported (open models, cheaper build) but the *measured* labour effect is single-digit-to-~20% speed-up with wide error bars — not enough on its own to collapse vertical software economics. The market repricing (Feb–Mar 2026) is anticipatory, not yet reflected in revenue (Atlassian still grew ~23%).
- Pricing is shifting from seats to outcomes; for an analytics vendor this means being paid per *validated decision* (per MWh forecast error avoided, per claim adjudicated) — which pushes the vendor toward bearing accountability (Zillow's failure mode) or being a feature of whoever does.
- Counter-evidence for the founder: incumbents with distribution (Salesforce, HubSpot, Intercom) are the ones rolling out outcome-priced agents; the compression hurts seat-based incumbents *and* removes the "build the UI" wedge that small vertical startups used to have.

- DORA 2025 quantitative findings not extracted.

## Key Question 7 — The "accumulated operational knowledge / customer-calibrated model" moat

### Takeaway
Vendor documentation and grid-operator procedure confirm that asset-specific calibration on customer SCADA is standard practice and that operators avoid frequent vendor switching — so stickiness is real. But (a) the calibration is on the *customer's* data, (b) operators deliberately run multiple vendors in parallel, and (c) the vendors that excelled at it (Solcast, Meteologica-type firms, AutoGrid) were acquired at undisclosed prices by incumbents. No retention/churn data was found for any vendor.

### Cited Findings
- Solcast: "physical down-scaling plus bias-correction/statistical learning"; "real-time SCADA is harvested via secure APIs to auto-update bias terms"; where SCADA unavailable, tunes on historical production data. — [Solcast Premium Power](https://solcast.com/premium-pv-wind-power-models) — **VF**.
- Meteologica: "accurate, site-specific power forecast for each asset", calibrated using generation and availability data. — [Meteologica services](https://meteologica.com/services.html) — **VF**.
- ERCOT procedure: do not "continuously switch between two wind forecasting services for a small gain in accuracy"; switch only on sustained, reliability-relevant under-performance with both vendors' accuracy logged; a specific named provider is the only permitted override for solar. — [ERCOT POB 1054](https://www.ercot.com/files/pobs/2022/08/31/Power_Operations_Bulletin_1054.doc) — **VF**. *(D: moat test "customer-specific calibration", "switching costs")*
- WindEurope 2026 poster: SCADA-calibrated wake models capture "site-, farm-, and turbine-specific effects" but face sensor-uncertainty challenges. — [WindEurope 2026 PO108](https://windeurope.org/annual2026/conference/posters/PO108) — **VF (abstract)**.
- IEA Wind Recommended Practice covers measurement selection, data QC and evaluation of forecasting processes — the standard an operator uses to benchmark and *replace* vendors. — [Elsevier](https://elsevier.com/books/iea-wind-recommended-practice-for-the-implementation-of-renewable-energy-forecasting-solutions/mohrlen/978-0-443-18681-3) — **VF (catalogue)**.
- Outcomes for calibration-centric vendors: Solcast → DNV (2023, undisclosed); AutoGrid → Schneider → Uplight (undisclosed); Spire Maritime → Kpler ($241M). (See KQ2/KQ3.)
- Counter-signal from frontier models: AlphaEarth and Chronos-2 are explicitly optimised for low-label / zero-shot regimes, shrinking the data volume needed to reach parity on a new asset. — [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphaearth-foundations-helps-map-our-planet-in-unprecedented-detail/); [arXiv 2510.15821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15821) — **VF**.

### Inferences
- Calibration stickiness exists but is *operational inertia*, not a proprietary asset: the SCADA belongs to the plant; a competitor given the same feed and a few months of history can match the bias correction, and foundation models are lowering that ramp.
- Multi-vendor procurement by ISOs/large IPPs (ERCOT runs ≥2 wind vendors) is the norm, which structurally caps price.
- The commercial ceiling for a calibration-only vendor appears to be an undisclosed-price acquisition by a certifier/OEM (DNV, Schneider).

### Gaps
- No vendor-level churn, NRR or contract-length data for solar/wind forecasting or O&M analytics was found.
- Indian QCA (Qualified Coordinating Agency) forecasting-market pricing and vendor switching — not searched (budget exhausted); **UNK**. This matters for a Bengaluru founder and should be the first follow-up.

Not one disclosed a price. Best source: [Uplight/AutoGrid release](https://uplight.com/press/uplight-to-acquire-autogrid/).

Best source: [Bichos de Campo](https://bichosdecampo.com/la-muerte-del-ojimetro-bayer-lanzo-una-plataforma-propia-para-incursionar-en-la-agricultura-digital) (EST) and [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing) (VF).

Best source: [Zillow 10-Q Q3 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764021000087/z-20210930.htm).

Best source: [Planet FY2026 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1836833/000119312526119957/pl-20260131.htm); [Via Satellite on BlackSky](https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2026/02/26/blackskys-growth-contracts-in-2025-for-second-year/).

Best source (EST-grade): [Monetizely on Airtable](https://www.getmonetizely.com/blogs/airtable-the-saaspocalypse-and-a-hail-mary); the labour-productivity side of "software is free" is only weakly measured ([METR, 2026-02-24](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)).

### Inferences
- The founder's thesis is correct about *where* value accrues (distribution, accountability, transaction access) and wrong about *who* captures it: in every case found, the capturer was an incumbent, a sensor owner, or a roll-up — never a software-only entrant assembling third-party physical-world data.
- If the founder still builds here, the evidence points to: (a) sell to one specific buyer who already has budget and accountability (an ISO, a DISCOM/QCA, an insurer, a certifier) and design for acquisition by them; (b) capture contractual rights to customer exhaust data rather than hoping calibration becomes a moat; (c) price per outcome from day one because seat pricing is being repriced out; (d) avoid any plan that requires government imagery/defence sales from India.

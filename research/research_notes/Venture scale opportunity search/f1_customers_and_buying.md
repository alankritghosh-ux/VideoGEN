## B. Buying process (who feels it, who pays, how they buy today, procurement path, cycle, blockers)

### Takeaway
Evidence is indirect: developers today buy connectivity help as (i) law-firm litigation when something has already gone wrong (JSA, K&S), (ii) regulatory consultants for petitions/transmission studies (Idam, Mercados), and (iii) in-house regulatory/BD teams at large IPPs that write their own CERC comment letters (Greenko, Zelestra). No evidence of any software line-item for this; a ₹2–10 lakh/yr tool sits below most mid-size IPPs' board-approval thresholds and is plausibly bought on a BD/regulatory head's discretionary budget, but this is inference.

## C. Evidence of the cost of the problem

### Takeaway
2026 primary/secondary records put hard rupee numbers on connectivity failure: BG encashments of ₹3.1–11.5 Cr per developer, compensation deposits totalling ₹19.26 Cr for three ReNew/Serentica SPVs, a published daily compensation rate (₹1,612/MW/day), proposed Milestone Extension Charges of ₹1,500–6,000/MW/day, Land-BG of ₹10 lakh/MW, and ~22 GW of stranded connectivity that must be resolved within a 60-day window. The 4th-amendment EM itself records that regulation-mandated timelines "could not be adhered to" and proposes letting CTUIL close applications.

### Cited Findings
- Land-BG route: BG of ₹10 lakh/MW up to 1,000 MW; ₹100 Cr + ₹5 lakh/MW above — Reg 5.8(xi)(c), quoted in [CERC EM GNA 4th Amendment, p.~21](https://www.cercind.gov.in/2026/draft_reg/EM_GNA-fourth-Amnd.pdf) **[VF]**. Same EM: withdrawal of application "with forfeiture of 50% application fee"; proposal "enabling CTUIL to close the connectivity applications where the transmission system … cannot be finalised", with BGs returned; stakeholders argued "Retaining Bank Guarantees (BGs) for a period of five years would significantly constrain" developers; ~40 GW of BGs referenced against approvals to 1 Jan 2028 (line 189 of extracted text, context partial) **[VF]**.
- Proposed Milestone Extension Charges (Petition 5/SM/2026, comments due 30 Apr 2026): land docs ₹1,500/MW/day (rising 10–20% months 2–3); FC ₹1,500→1,950/MW/day over 6 months; COD ₹3,000/MW/day months 1–6, up to ₹3,900 months 7–9, ₹6,000 months 10–12; overall extension capped at 12 months; 2-month grace after GNA effective date — [Power Peak Digest 16 Apr 2026](https://powerpeakdigest.com/cerc-proposes-compensation-charges-for-gna-timeline-extensions/) **[IC]**. ## F. Customer-interview script (behaviour, cost, authority, urgency — not idea-liking)

Target: head of BD / regulatory affairs / project development at Tier-2 IPPs on the CTUIL 11/SM/2026 list; 2 lenders/advisors. 30 minutes. Record who answered; do not pitch until Q13.

1. Walk me through the last connectivity application you filed: who chose the substation, what data did you use, and how long from decision to NSWS submission?
2. In the last 24 months, how many of your applications moved from in-principle to firm on the original date? What slipped, and by how long?
3. When CTUIL publishes a new bay-margin / solar–non-solar-hour sheet, cluster declaration or reallocation notice, who in your company sees it, within how many days, and how?
4. Has any BG of yours been encashed, or compensation/MEC paid, since 2024? Amount, cause, and what internal change followed?
5. Did you receive a deficiency letter or revocation notice with less than 15 days to respond? What did it cost you (people-days, counsel fees, BG)?
6. Are you on the 11/SM/2026 eligible list? Which of the options did you elect, and what analysis did you do before choosing?
7. Who did you pay externally for connectivity work in the last two years — law firm, consultant, individual advisor? Approximate fee per engagement and per year?
8. What does your in-house team spend per month on tracking CTUIL/CERC/RLDC publications (headcount × share of time)?
9. If a tool reduced that to alerts plus a ranked substation shortlist, which budget would it come from, who signs below ₹5 lakh, and what vendor-onboarding steps apply?
10. Have you bought any subscription data/software for the business in the last year (e.g., tender trackers, market data)? Price paid, who approved, how long it took?
11. For your next bid, which three substations are you considering and why? What would change your mind?
12. If you could know one thing CTUIL won't tell you, what is it? (Expect: when transmission for X will be ready; who else is queued at Y; probability of reallocation.)
13. (Only now) Here is a one-page sample for substation Z. What is wrong or missing? What would you not trust?
14. Would you pay ₹1–3 lakh for a six-week pilot covering your live applications? If not, what would have to be true?
15. Who else inside or outside your company would need to agree, and who would object?

Pass criteria mirror decision_brief Experiment B: ≥4/10 pay an external party today or lost ≥₹1 Cr in 24 months, AND ≥3 agree to a paid pilot.

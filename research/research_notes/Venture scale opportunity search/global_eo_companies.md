## Key question 1: What imagery can a startup access for free or cheaply today, under what licence, at what price?

### Takeaway
A software-only startup can build on genuinely free, commercially-usable data: Copernicus Sentinel-1/2 (EU law grants reproduction, distribution, adaptation and commercial use with attribution), Landsat (no restrictions), Umbra SAR open data (CC BY 4.0, 25 cm-class, ~20 time-series sites), and Capella SAR open data (CC BY 4.0). High-resolution optical (Vantor/Maxar open data) is **non-commercial only** and disaster-event-only, and Planet's free tropical mosaics (NICFI) ended January 2025. Cheap paid access exists: SkyFi from $15 per archive image / $5 per km² optical archive / $450 per SAR scene; Umbra tasking from $675 per 5x5 km 1 m scene; SkySat 50 cm ~$10-12 per km² via resellers (25 km² minimum); Planet's self-serve PlanetScope monitoring tiers from $2,700/yr (search snapshot, marked "Coming Soon").

**Copernicus Sentinel-1/2 (free, full, open; commercial use allowed)**
- The EU "Legal notice on the use of Copernicus Sentinel Data and Service Information" states users have "free, full and open access" and grants use for "(a) reproduction; (b) distribution; (c) communication to the public; (d) adaptation, modification and combination with other data and information; (e) any combination of points (a) to (d)." Required attribution: "'Copernicus Sentinel data [Year]'" and, for modified data, "'Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data [Year]'." Governed by Regulation (EU) No 377/2014 and Delegated Regulation (EU) No 1159/2013. — [Sentinel Data Legal Notice (PDF)](https://sentinels.copernicus.eu/documents/247904/690755/Sentinel_Data_Legal_Notice) | **VF** | undated PDF (regulation 2013/2014) | informs: a startup may legally build and sell commercial products on Sentinel data with attribution.

### Inferences
- The free, commercially-licensed stack for a startup is: Sentinel-1 (SAR, ~10 m, 6-12 day revisit), Sentinel-2 (optical, 10 m, ~5 day), Landsat (30 m, 16 day), plus small free high-res SAR samples (Umbra, Capella) for model training. Everything at <5 m optical costs money, and the cheapest reliable entry is SkyFi ($15-$200 per image) or Umbra direct ($675+ per SAR scene).
- Licence asymmetry matters: Sentinel/Landsat/Umbra/Capella open data allow commercial derivative products and even redistribution; Vantor open data and Planet E&R/NICFI do not. SkyFi allows selling analytics but not pixels.
- A startup should therefore position itself on derived information (change, counts, indices, alerts) rather than imagery resale — the licences push that way and so do the economics.

## Key question 2: Company-by-company — what each actually sells, to whom, with what capability, and which stack layer it occupies

### Takeaway
The operators that are healthy in 2026 (Planet, ICEYE, BlackSky, Vantor, Umbra, Capella-under-IonQ) are vertically integrated satellite owners whose revenue is dominated by defence/intelligence and sovereign-government buyers; the pure "analytics on other people's satellites" companies (Orbital Insight, Descartes Labs, Ursa, Kayrros) have all been absorbed by larger owners or remain small. Weather is a different pattern: Tomorrow.io and Spire moved from software into owning sensors to differentiate.

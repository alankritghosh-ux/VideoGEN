# Red-team review: hostile investment-committee examination of the leading opportunity

Prepared 2026-10-17 from the Phase C/D research notes only; no new research. Binding frame: `research_notes/Venture scale opportunity search/decision_brief.md` §7–§9 (final order F2 71 · F1 63 · F3 60; "Validate First on F2"). Evidence classes follow the notes: **VF** verified fact (primary document read), **IC** independently corroborated, **EST** estimate, **HYP** hypothesis, **UNK** unknown. Disagreements with the brief are recorded in the Dissent box in §8; the ranking is not changed.

## 1. Mandate and method

Three finalists were red-teamed at finalist depth: F1 (ISTS connectivity intelligence), F2 (C&I open-access settlement and procurement engine) and F3 (FDRE/BESS dispatch and bid optimisation), each against seven hostile questions (incumbent bundling, weak-demand explanations, frontier-model reproducibility, cheaper substitutes, data-licensing risk, regulatory timing, strongest reason not to build) plus a five-assumption table and recomputed scores, on 25 searches and ~35 primary fetches (`red_team_counterevidence.md`). F1 fell 77→63 and F3 72→60 while F2 fell only 76→71, which created a fairness problem: three hypotheses never attacked (H4 CBAM 67, H5 dev-intel 64, H6 CCTS 64) now sat above two red-teamed finalists on untouched scores. The fix was a second bounded red team of that cluster at the same depth (15 searches, ~40 fetches, four Gazette PDFs and two EU regulations read in full; `red_team_cluster2.md`), which cut each by 9–14 points (H4 53, H6 55, H5 53). The brief then fixed the convention this review follows: every remaining un-red-teamed score is shown "raw" with an expected haircut of about 10 points, and no un-red-teamed hypothesis ranks above a red-teamed finalist. The method's structural weakness, stated up front: none of the sixteen hypotheses has a single customer interview behind it; every willingness-to-pay cell is desk-derived.

## 2. The leading opportunity, as its advocate would put it, then attacked

The advocate's case. C&I green open access passed 30 GW by December 2025 (industry estimate, EST) from an 18.7 GW base growing at 46% CAGR to FY2024 ([JMK/IEEFA, IC](https://jmkresearch.com/renewable-sector-published-reports/impact-of-green-energy-open-access-rules-2022/)); Karnataka alone holds 23% of India's 32.9 GW of OA solar ([pv magazine citing Mercom, IC](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/05/22/india-has-now-32-9-gw-of-operational-pv-capacity-under-private-ppas/)). Every OA consumer receives two bills a month, generator and DISCOM ([OAE, VF](https://www.openaccessexchange.com/)), plus SLDC energy-accounting and banking statements, under a charge stack the regulator re-sets yearly: BESCOM asked for ₹1.65/kWh of additional surcharge on a ₹1,028 Cr stranded-cost claim and KERC approved ₹0.40 against ₹0.82 in force, exempting captive drawal ([SolarQuarter, VF](https://solarquarter.com/2025/10/14/bescom-seeks-kerc-approval-to-levy-%e2%82%b91-65-per-unit-additional-surcharge-on-open-access-consumers-in-karnataka/); [Mercom, IC](https://mercomindia.com/karnataka-halves-additional-surcharge-for-open-access-power)). Karnataka runs two banking regimes at once: 2% annual in-kind for 2018–2023 applicants versus monthly, no carry-forward, under the 2025 regulations ([SolarQuarter 2023, VF](https://solarquarter.com/2023/08/17/kerc-issues-vital-directive-on-wheeling-charges-and-banking-for-renewable-power-projects/); [SolarQuarter 2025, VF-secondary](https://solarquarter.com/2025/04/02/karnataka-introduces-new-open-access-regulations-2025-to-boost-power-accessibility-and-renewable-energy/)). Over-billing is documented: MERC ordered MSEDCL to refund unlawfully collected wheeling charges in 2025 ([MERC order, VF-exists](https://merc.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Final-Order-in-Case-Nos.-131-of-2024.pdf)); APTEL recorded a commission directing a utility "to correct the bills of 23 open access consumers" ([APTEL 366/2017, single-source](https://www.aptel.gov.in/judgements/Judg2018/A.No.%20366%20of%202017%20&%20IA%20No.%20933%20of%202017_28.11.18_J.pdf)). The prospect list is public: Karnataka SLDC publishes 556 generators on 4,924 MW of wheeling-and-banking agreements and roughly 922 distinct consumer organisations, Infosys to Shahi Exports to Biocon, mostly within 40 km of a Bengaluru founder ([KPTCL SLDC, VF count / EST dedupe](https://kptclsldc.in/WBUpload.aspx)). A product that ingests the consumer's own exports, recomputes what it should have paid, recovers the difference on a success fee and then sells annual sourcing optimisation needs no privileged data and validates on twelve months of one customer's bills. Nobody on Tracxn's 205-company trading/storage feed does it.

The attack. Four named incumbents already sell parts of this job inside a transaction they hold; no Indian fee for OA bill audit or advisory exists in print; the documented over-billing was corrected by commission orders for dozens of consumers at once, for free; the consumer's statements have not been shown to be exportable; and the only moats that held in the physical-world-data record belong to whoever owns distribution or the transaction, here the trader or developer, not the auditor. F2 is the best of a weak set, not a proven business.

## 4. The thirteen hostile questions, answered for F2

**(a) Could a general-purpose frontier model reproduce the core functionality?** The recompute layer, yes: tariff orders are text and the MSEDCL OA bill itemises drawal, injection, banked and over-injected units ([MSEDCL sample, VF](https://mahadiscom.in/consumer/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/155-519_170019002163_202411-oa-print1.pdf)). What a model cannot reproduce is the binding interpretation, which is a commission decision: MERC's 2021 proceeding exists because the netting order of RE credits against scheduled power was ambiguous, and APTEL 366/2017 turned on a DISCOM mis-applying its own credit mechanism. The non-reproducible asset is the rule library (which of Karnataka's two banking regimes applies to a consumer-generator pair, keyed to WBA dates in the SLDC spreadsheet) plus bill/settlement pairs and dispute outcomes per DISCOM, which require contractual rights to customer data (`red_team_counterevidence.md` Q3). The parse layer is a week's work and no moat; the rule-plus-outcome library is the product.

**(b) Could an incumbent bundle it?** It is already bundled: TPTCL with trading margin and SLDC coordination; CES with a CERC trading licence (Sl. 51, [CERC list, VF-stale](https://cercind.gov.in/Electric-Trading/List_of_firms_who_have_been_granted_the_inter_state_trading_licence_in_electcity.pdf)); OAE with developer-subsidised procurement; BridgeIT with multi-site bill validation (OA logic unconfirmed; four employees listed, EST). DISCOM portals print itemised OA bills; SLDC energy-accounting software sits behind Maharashtra's REMC/DSM portals ([MSLDC, VF-menus](https://mahasldc.in/)). The one incumbent weakness in the notes is a conflict of interest, a trader auditing trades it arranged (`f2_f3_commercial_facts.md` F2-Q4). A real wedge, but a positioning claim, not evidence that consumers care.

**(c) Could the customer use an analyst, consultant or spreadsheet?** Today, yes: two bills, a tariff order and Excel, or CES on retainer. The counter-trend is prospective: Karnataka's 2025 rules settle banking monthly and bill standby, under-utilisation and relinquishment separately ([Renewable Watch, IC](https://renewablewatch.in/2025/04/25/karnataka-announces-open-access-regulations-2025/)); Punjab's draft moves banking to 15-minute blocks ([SolarQuarter, VF](https://solarquarter.com/2025/11/19/punjab-state-power-corporation-introduces-clear-banking-draft-procedure-to-support-green-energy-open-access-consumers-in-punjab/)); MERC has capacity-based banking out for comment ([Mercom, IC](https://mercomindia.com/maharashtra-proposes-capacity-based-banking-for-renewable-open-access)). Block-level banking across multiple generator pairings (Shahi Exports: ≥6 HT installations) is where the spreadsheet breaks; until then it wins on price.

**(d) Is it merely a wrapper around accessible data?** The public inputs are free and the private inputs belong to the customer, so the danger is inverted: F2 wraps the customer's own data, which the customer already has. The commercial model concedes the only variable costs are support hours, compute and customer-supplied data (`commercial-model-summary.md`). Value must sit in the rule library and the recovery track record or there is no product.

**(e) Is the problem frequent and costly enough for a recurring purchase?** Unproven. Costly events are episodic (regime changes, surcharge swings, ordered refunds); the frequent event is monthly accounting, which may be bookkeeping. The pool is arithmetic: BESCOM's petition implies ~6,230 MU/yr of OA energy (₹1,028 Cr ÷ ₹1.65/kWh, EST), so a ₹0.10/kWh error rate would be ₹62 Cr/yr across BESCOM alone (EST). The error rate is UNK, which is what F2-A measures; a fail (<0.5% at 4 of 5 sites) reduces F2 to procurement optimisation, where the free CEEW calculator, frozen at FY22 tariffs ([CEEW, VF](https://www.ceew.in/cef/shared/CEEW-CEF-Open-access-landed-cost-calculator-methodology.pdf)), and developer advisory compete.

**(f) Does it require data the founder cannot obtain?** Yes, and no note verified the consumer can supply it. The brief scores Data 4 because bills are "routine exports", but the SLDC energy-accounting and banking statements are the settlement-critical inputs, and no Karnataka public page shows them. If the generator's QCA, not the consumer, holds them, the founder needs the developer's cooperation, which brings A5 back.

**(g) Can the customer verify the result?** For a bill correction, yes: the credit appears on the next bill, which is why success-fee pricing is credible. For a claim needing a petition, verification is years away (APTEL decided 366/2017 in November 2018), so the fee is unbilled cash: the model bills ₹13.3 lakh in year one and collects ₹4.05 lakh because recovery cash arrives after a 6-month lag plus DSO (`commercial-model-summary.md`, HYP inputs). Verification also cuts against the vendor: ~₹4.5 Cr/yr of sign-off liability at 1,000 customers on ₹225 Cr of claimed recoveries at 2% misstatement.

**(h) Is the apparent gap evidence of weak demand?** The hostile reading: the market grew from 18.7 GW to ~30 GW with no settlement vendor appearing because the developers and traders who hold the transaction give the work away to win and keep it; OAE's "zero upfront", Schneider's free advisory, Hero FE's undisclosed advisory fee and TPTCL's bundled accounting are one pattern. Against this: JMK/IEEFA found banking and settlement periods "still ambiguous across states" and the GOAR portal lacking SLDC–DISCOM synchronisation (IC), and MERC's refunds prove DISCOMs over-bill. Errors are real, and the parties positioned to catch them are already in the transaction.

**(i) What if the data becomes free or the model dramatically better?** Tariff-order reading is already free. The exposure is upstream: if KERC/SLDC publish machine-readable energy accounts (KPTCL lists an "FTC Procedure – June 2026", VF) or DISCOMs bill correctly, the recompute layer is worth nothing. Data owners do price analytics at zero to defend the core business: Bayer cut FieldView from $350/yr to $49/yr ([EST](https://bichosdecampo.com/la-muerte-del-ojimetro-bayer-lanzo-una-plataforma-propia-para-incursionar-en-la-agricultura-digital)). What survives is multi-state regime identification and procurement optimisation, the DERS-versus-OA decision KERC reopened for every HT consumer on 31 Mar 2026 ([SolarQuarter, VF](https://solarquarter.com/2026/04/02/kerc-extends-discounted-energy-rate-scheme-till-fy28-eases-open-access-rules-for-consumers-in-karnataka/)).

**(j) What defensible advantage accumulates in 12–24 months?** A Karnataka rule library covering both banking regimes, N audited bill-sets with outcomes, and a CA-signed recovery record. By §7's moat test that is hygiene, not pricing power. The model's sensitivity says payback stays under 4 months across price and win-rate grids and that "F2's economics are not the risk; segment reach, incumbent bundling and the unmeasured recoverable % are". Advantage accumulates only if data rights are contractual and the vendor moves toward the transaction position (§7).

**(k) The strongest reason not to build.** Four incumbents bundle settlement at no visible fee into a transaction they hold, no fee benchmark exists, and documented over-billing is corrected in bulk by regulators, so the buyer has a free substitute and no price anchor, and the vendor has no transaction position (`red_team_counterevidence.md` Q7).

**(l) Regulatory timing.** Karnataka's regime is in churn: the High Court struck down the 2022 GEOA rules, the 2025 regulations replaced them, BESCOM contests the surcharge annually, and KERC's CSS roadmap dates from 7 Mar 2026 ([Energetica, VF](https://www.energetica-india.net/news/kerc-issues-2026-regulations-notifies-roadmap-to-gradually-reduce-cross-subsidy-and-cross-subsidy-surcharge)). Tamil Nadu's wind-banking rate has been re-litigated at least four times; APTEL set aside TNERC's hike again in June 2025 ([Prayas, VF](https://energy.prayaspune.org/power-perspectives/caught-in-a-whirlwind-a-commentary-on-competing-interests-and-regulatory-lacunae-in-the-provision-of-wind-energy-banking-in-tamil-nadu)). The DSM stays (Karnataka HC for NSEFI petitioners, [PowerPeak, VF-secondary](https://powerpeakdigest.com/karnataka-hc-stays-cerc-dsm-2024/); Delhi HC interim orders in force, [single secondary](https://advocategandhi.com/?p=41926)) mainly affect generators but show penalty regimes suspended, not enforced. MERC's refunds are double-edged: errors exist, and the regulator fixes them without an auditor. Churn is the recurring need F2 sells and the reason the model needs three state rule libraries at 100 customers and six at 1,000.

**(m) Legal and data risks.** Customer bill data raises no third-party privacy issue identified, but no DISCOM terms were examined (UNK) and SLDC commercial-reuse rules were searched and not found (`red_team_counterevidence.md` Q5). MSLDC requires prior permission even to hyperlink ([MSLDC, VF](https://mahasldc.in/website-policies)); KPTCL SLDC shows no terms. Using SLDC consumer lists with RR numbers for outbound prospecting is unexamined under the DPDP Act; organisations are not data principals so risk is low (HYP), but nobody asked. The larger exposure is advice liability: a CA-signed recomputed bill that is wrong is the vendor's liability, and Zillow (§7) is what bearing that risk looks like.

## 6. Why the un-red-teamed cluster fell

**H4 CBAM (67 → 53).** The obligation sits with the EU importer, who may lawfully declare on default values with no verification: Regulation (EU) 2025/2083 recital 57, "The verification of embedded emissions should therefore only apply to actual values" ([EUR-Lex, VF](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R2083)), and Implementing Regulation 2025/2621 expects declarants to "use default values in those first years" ([EUR-Lex, VF](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32025R2621)). The importer's ERP now ingests supplier Excel ([SAP, VF](https://news.sap.com/2026/06/sap-introduces-cbam-declarants-solution/)), the Omnibus removed finishing-step emissions (hollowing the re-roller wedge), the free-allocation adjustment scales 2026–27 cash down (factor unverified, EST), and EU-bound flat steel is expected to fall from July 2026 as safeguard quotas halve ([Argus, VF](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2817945-india-becomes-net-steel-exporter-but-outlook-uncertain)). Real money, 2028+, reachable only through verifiers and ERPs.

**H6 CCTS (64 → 55).** Nothing to comply against yet. G.S.R. 739(E) rule 6 sets compensation at "twice of the average price at which carbon credit certificate is traded" ([Gazette via BEE, VF](https://www.beeindia.gov.in/WriteReadData/L45218/1663540214634949.pdf)); as of 7 Oct 2026 no CCC had traded and no floor or ceiling existed ([Carbonneeti, VF-vendor](https://carbonneeti.com/blog/india-ccts-compliance-deadlines-2026); [crbn.credit, IC](https://crbn.credit/post/india-carbon-credit-certificate-trading-october-2026)). BEE's verifier bench on 25 Mar 2026 was six provisional energy-audit firms for ~490 plants ([BEE letter, VF](https://www.beeindia.gov.in/WriteReadData/L45218/6065726594257663.pdf)); PAT's precedent is 56% non-compliance and floor-price-only trades ([Prayas, VF](https://energy.prayaspune.org/power-perspectives/pat-iii-new-cycle-old-issues)). Best data-legality profile in the set; a 2027+ option.

**H5 dev-intel (64 → 53).** The records layer is already sold at ₹3,390 per seat per year by Blackridge, whose trial form's budget bracket starts at "Under $5,000" ([Blackridge, VF](https://www.blackridgeresearch.com/new-projects-near-me/solar-photovoltaic-pv-database/c/India/subscribe)), by Mercom and Crisil-Bridge to India at undisclosed prices, and given away by GEM at 1 MW+ with CEA plant lists as a source, CC BY 4.0 except TransitionZero rows at CC BY-NC ([GEM, VF](https://globalenergymonitor.org/projects/global-solar-power-tracker/)). The only differentiator, satellite stage verification, carries the fee economics of rejected H11 (0.01–0.02% of capex) and no Indian buyer was found.

## 10. Verdict

Does any opportunity survive? None survives as a "Build". No finalist has verified willingness-to-pay for software; every WTP cell is desk-derived from proxies (report prices, mandated spend, the existence of consultants), and the moat record says value in this category accrues to whoever owns distribution or the transaction, which none of the finalists starts with.

F2 survives as the best-evidenced candidate for a Validate-First path, on three structural points the red team could not dislodge: its data arrives as the customer's own exports with no redistribution problem; its buyers are numerous, named in public SLDC lists and local to Bengaluru; and billing errors are documented by regulators. It leads a probe (F1) by seven points and a watch item (F3) by eleven; under the Dissent's stricter scoring the margin is four. Enough to spend a founder's time on, not a rupee of outside capital.

Kill criteria, applied in the order the experiments return:

- **Kill on data (F2-A, step one):** fewer than 3 of 5 volunteer consumers can produce twelve months of bills plus SLDC/ESCOM energy-accounting statements within 14 days; stop, or re-scope to the generator/QCA side where RPC files are public.
- **Kill on money (F2-A):** median recoverable (bill corrections plus avoidable banking-lapse, standby and under-utilisation losses) below 0.5% of the annual OA bill at 4 of 5 sites; or, at the pass threshold (≥1.5% and ≥₹5 lakh/yr at ≥3 sites), more than half of the value is litigation-only.
- **Kill on demand (F2-B):** fewer than 2 of 10 consumers pay anyone, in cash or in bundled fees they can name, for OA settlement, and none accepts a success-fee audit in writing.
- **Kill on position:** F2-B shows the developer or trader, not the consumer, holds the statements and makes the settlement decisions; do not build a consumer dashboard, sell through the developer or stop.
- **Kill on incumbency:** TPTCL, CES or a top-five OA developer confirms a standalone consumer-side reconciliation service at a disclosed price below ₹2 lakh/site/yr; the independent-auditor wedge is priced out.

If all five hold, the next gate is not "Build" but one paid pilot with a multi-installation consumer (Shahi Exports, RMZ or Bagmane class) on outcome pricing with exhaust-data rights in the engagement letter, with the F1 permission email and the H16 RPC-file probe running in parallel at zero cost.
